**Framing question from Austin:** internal scores are high-70s to high-80s, the
hiring-manager sim advances 17 of 32 packets, yet the market signal is completely
flat. Where is the disconnect, and what is the highest-leverage fix to actually

**Short answer, and it is not the answer anyone wants:** the premise of the

## 0. The headline finding: the outcome data does not say what it looks like it says

### 0.1 It is 14 submitted applications, not 39

`logs/applications_log.csv` has 39 rows. Those are **pipeline runs**, not
applications. It logs every invocation, including re-runs of the same job:
Trivelta appears 3 times, Fivetran 3 times, Clerkie twice, NeuroHire twice,
"Software Engineer" twice on 2026-07-26.

`~/Documents/Resume Applications/Application Tracker.xlsx` — which Austin
maintains by hand and which `src/tracker.py` upserts into — has 26 unique
company/role rows with an `Application Status` column:

| Status | Count |
|---|---|
| Applied | 12 |
| Rejected | 2 |
| Gone (posting closed before applying) | 3 |
| **Not Applied** | **9** |

**Real submitted applications: 14.** The last one was Waymark on **2026-08-11**,
eleven days ago. Every one of the nine runs since 2026-08-13 (Omniscient,
Roboflow, SMX, Logical, Wisevu, Runpod, Harness, Camber, Google) is marked
**Not Applied**.

### 0.2 Zero callbacks from 14 applications is the single most likely outcome

Convergent benchmark data puts cold application-to-interview conversion at
**2–3%**, with tech roles at the lower end and job-board applications lower still
([Pin recruitment funnel benchmarks](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-funnel-benchmarks/),
[ResuTrack 2026 benchmarks](https://resutrack.com/blog/how-many-job-applications-to-get-interview-2026),
[onehour.digital funnel statistics](https://onehour.digital/blog/job-application-funnel-statistics)).

At n=14:

| True conversion rate | Expected callbacks | P(zero callbacks) |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | 0.28 | **75%** |
| 3% | 0.42 | **65%** |
| 5% | 0.70 | 49% |
| 8.4% (optimistic) | 1.18 | 29% |

Even at the *optimistic* end, zero callbacks from 14 applications happens 29% of
the time. At the realistic 2–3% baseline it happens about **two times in three**.

**This is not evidence that the system is broken.** It is not evidence that the
system works either — 14 samples cannot distinguish a 1% resume from a 5% resume.
The honest statement is: *there is no diagnostic signal in the outcome data yet.*

I want to be very direct with Austin about this, because the framing "39
applications, 0 callbacks, something must be catastrophically wrong" leads to
exactly the wrong response — another round of prompt surgery on a system that is
already the most over-engineered part of this pipeline. The three defects I found
below are real and worth fixing. But **the single highest-leverage action is not
in this codebase: it is submitting the nine finished packets sitting in
`output/` marked Not Applied, and raising throughput to a level where the
outcome data becomes readable at all** (~50–75 submissions before a zero-callback
result means anything).

### 0.3 On the Automattic 4am-Saturday rejection specifically

Austin's read (automated bulk filter, not human review) is almost certainly
correct, and the mechanism is most likely a **knockout question**, not resume
parsing. Knockout questions are binary pre-screen filters on the application
form — work authorization, licenses, a hard years-of-experience minimum — and
they eliminate an application with zero human review; instant or overnight
rejections are their signature
([Jobscan on knockout questions](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/knockout-questions-answer-application/),
[jobsearch.guide](https://newsletter.jobsearch.guide/p/knockout-questions)).
Automattic also runs an unusually high-volume, trial-based funnel.

Practical implication: an 85/100 internal score and an "advance" verdict tell you
nothing about a knockout filter, because the packet never got read. **This is a
category of failure the pipeline cannot see and should stop being asked to
explain.** Recommendation R7 below adds the one thing that would help.

### R1 — CRITICAL — **prompt/process fix** — The company/title line has no delimiter, and that is an ATS parsing failure on every resume ever generated

**This is the highest-confidence mechanical defect in the system.**

Two rules in this codebase are in direct contradiction:

`src/draft_agent.py`, SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS, Experience section:
> "Each entry is a single line: company name in **bold**, then **an em dash**,
> then title + dates in *italics*"

`src/draft_agent.py`, ~45 lines later, Writing style:
> "Never use an em dash (—)."

...and `src/style_lint.py` flags every em dash as a `medium` issue that consumes
a revision pass. The model resolves the contradiction the only way it can: **it
drops the separator entirely.**

Verified against the actual shipped `.docx` (`output/runpod-.../resume.docx`,
extracted paragraph text):

```
'Prospecta Marketing Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive - Feb 2024-Present'
'Tangent Apps (Independent Consulting) Freelance Software Developer - Mar 2021-Present'
```

There is **no delimiter at all** between employer and job title. 24 of 33
generated resumes have this exact shape (`**Prospecta Marketing** *Senior SEO
Specialist...*` with nothing between the bold and italic runs, which renders as a
single unbroken string).

Why this matters more than anything else in this audit — three independent
sources converge:

1. **Parsers segment on line breaks and delimiters.** "Line breaks help
   segmentation; inconsistent company/location layouts confuse Taleo
   specifically." Taleo's segmenter has been observed to treat a mis-formatted
   second job "as a continuation of the first," dropping the entry entirely
   ([Resume Optimizer Pro — how parsers actually work](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/how-resume-parsers-actually-work)).
2. **The recommended layout is multi-line.** "Line 1: company name. Line 2:
   title. Line 3: dates and location." Reversing to title-first "breaks Workday
   parsing on roughly 15 percent of resumes"
   ([FastApply ATS format guide 2026](https://blog.fastapply.co/ats-resume-format-guide-2026)).
3. **The extracted JSON is what recruiters actually search.** "That JSON is what
   the application form autofills and what recruiters query when they filter by
   'Python' or 'Senior Product Manager.' **They are not searching the original
   resume.**" (Resume Optimizer Pro, above; corroborated by
   [Scale.jobs on ATS filtering](https://scale.jobs/blog/how-recruiters-filter-resumes-using-ats-insider-breakdown)
   and [LinkedIn Recruiter Boolean docs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295)).

Compounding it: the date format. Convergent guidance is `MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY`
with spaces; `2023-2025` and unspaced ranges are listed as **failing** formats.
Every resume currently emits `Feb 2024-Present` and `Mar 2021-Present` — no
spaces around the hyphen.

**Exact fix — `src/draft_agent.py`, replace the Experience-format paragraph:**

> "Experience section: '### Professional Experience' heading. Each entry uses
> **two lines**, in this exact order, because ATS parsers segment employment
> entries on line breaks and a single run-on line causes the employer and title
> to be extracted as one field:
>
> Line 1: company name in `**bold**`, on its own line, nothing else.
> Line 2: title, then ` | `, then dates, in `*italics*`.
>
> Example:
> ```
> **Prospecta Marketing**
> *Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive | Feb 2024 - Present*
> ```
>
> Dates must always use the `Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY` shape with a **space on both
> sides of the hyphen** and a spelled-out three-letter month, and `Present` for
> current roles. Never `Feb 2024-Present`, never `2024-2026`, never a slash
> format. Never use an em dash anywhere, including here — the separator between
> title and dates is a pipe with spaces (` | `), and there is no separator
> between company and title because they are on different lines."

**Exact fix — `data/resume_best_practices.md` §Section Structure item 4:** replace
"Each entry is a single line: **company name** in bold, then an em dash, then
*title - dates* in italics ... (company leads, not title — this one-line format
replaces the old two-line company/title layout to save vertical space)" with the
same two-line spec above, and delete the "to save vertical space" justification.
That trade was made against an unstated assumption that vertical space is scarcer
than parseability. It is not: the runpod resume renders at one page with room to
spare, and `length_fit.py` already handles overflow.

**Exact fix — `src/structure_lint.py`, add a new deterministic check** (this rule
has now been ignored in both directions by prompt alone; it needs a backstop):

```python
_ENTRY_LINE_RE = re.compile(r"^\*\*(?P<company>[^*]+)\*\*\s*(?P<rest>.*)$")
_DATE_OK_RE = re.compile(
    r"\b[A-Z][a-z]{2} \d{4} - (?:[A-Z][a-z]{2} \d{4}|Present)\b"
)

def _check_experience_entry_format(resume: str) -> list[dict]:
    """ATS parsers segment employment entries on line breaks and extract
    employer/title/dates as separate fields; a run-on company+title line
    with no delimiter is extracted as one blob and makes the candidate
    unfindable in a recruiter title filter. Deterministic because the
    prompt rule contradicted the em-dash ban and the model resolved it by
    dropping the separator on 24 of 33 real runs."""
    issues = []
    exp = re.search(r"### Professional Experience\n(.*?)(?=\n### |\Z)",
                    resume, re.DOTALL)
    if not exp:
        return issues
    for line in exp.group(1).splitlines():
        m = _ENTRY_LINE_RE.match(line.strip())
        if not m:
            continue
        rest = m.group("rest").strip()
        if rest:
            issues.append({
                "category": "ATS - Experience Entry Format",
                "detail": (
                    f"Company line also carries the title/dates on the same "
                    f"line: {line.strip()!r}. ATS parsers segment employment "
                    "entries on line breaks; company must be alone on its own "
                    "line with the italicized 'Title | Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY' "
                    "line directly beneath it."
                ),
                "severity": "high",
            })
    for m in re.finditer(r"\b([A-Z][a-z]{2} \d{4}\s*[-–—]\s*(?:[A-Z][a-z]{2} \d{4}|Present))",
                         exp.group(1)):
        if not _DATE_OK_RE.search(m.group(1)):
            issues.append({
                "category": "ATS - Date Format",
                "detail": (
                    f"Date range {m.group(1)!r} is not in the parser-safe "
                    "'Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY' form with a space on both sides of "
                    "the hyphen. Unspaced and en/em-dash ranges are documented "
                    "parse failures."
                ),
                "severity": "medium",
            })
    return issues
```

Wire into `lint_draft()` alongside the existing checks.

**Expected impact:** this is the only finding in the audit that could plausibly
prevent a *technically qualified* application from ever being seen. It costs
nothing to fix.

### R2 — CRITICAL — **prompt/process fix** — Tangent Apps client work is being attributed to Prospecta, on 6 resumes and 10 cover letters

`src/structure_lint.py::_check_employer_attribution` was added after a real
shipped bug (Jeff Gray, DDS placed under Tangent Apps). It only checks **one
direction**: Prospecta clients appearing inside the Tangent Apps block. The
reverse — a Tangent Apps client appearing inside the Prospecta block — has
**never been checked, and has shipped far more often than the bug that prompted
the original fix.**

Scan results (Vocation Action Network work appearing inside the Prospecta entry):

| Application | Evidence |
|---|---|
| hims-hers-2026-08-04 | Names it outright: "Architected and shipped a full-stack WordPress plugin (PHP/MySQL) **for Vocation Action Network**, a branch of an international religious organization" — inside the Prospecta entry |
| smx-2026-08-19 | "Built two full-stack WordPress plugins from scratch for a client organization with a membership base in the millions: a **prayer-tracking** system..." — inside Prospecta |
| wisevu-2026-08-22 | "Built two full-stack WordPress plugins from scratch **as this agency's primary developer** for one of its client accounts, a religious organization..." — inside Prospecta, **and the same work also appears under Tangent Apps on the same page** |
| google-2026-08-08 | membership-in-the-millions + prayer-logging inside Prospecta |
| camber-2026-08-09 | prayer-logging inside Prospecta |
| neurohire-2026-07-26 | prayer-logging inside Prospecta |

Plus 10 cover letters, including the most recent (**runpod-2026-08-22**):
"at Prospecta Marketing ..., I built two full-stack WordPress plugins from
scratch, custom database tables, a REST-style AJAX API, and an admin dashboard,
for an organization with a membership base in the millions."

The wisevu case is the worst: the same plugin build is claimed under two
different employers on one page. That is visibly incoherent to a human reader
*and* it is exactly the class of misstatement the master employer-attribution
Calibration Note exists to prevent.

**Exact fix — `src/structure_lint.py`, add the mirror check:**

```python
_TANGENT_ONLY_CLIENTS = [
    "vocation action network", "regnum christi", "dnvr", "phnx",
    "all city media", "lacroix", "drill house",
    "ahead of the curve", "aotc", "honeycomb strategies",
]
# Descriptive phrases that identify a Tangent Apps client without naming it —
# these shipped inside the Prospecta entry on 5 real resumes with the client
# name stripped out, so a name-only list would not have caught them.
_TANGENT_ONLY_MARKERS = [
    "prayer", "membership base in the millions", "millions of members",
    "religious organization", "sports media startup", "youth hockey",
    "colorado avalanche",
]

def _check_reverse_employer_attribution(resume: str, cover_letter: str) -> list[dict]:
    """Mirror of _check_employer_attribution. Tangent Apps freelance client
    work being described inside the Prospecta entry misstates which company
    the work was performed for — same integrity class as the original bug,
    and it has shipped on 6 resumes and 10 cover letters while only the
    opposite direction was checked."""
    issues = []
    m = re.search(r"\*\*Prospecta Marketing\*\*(.*?)(?=\n\*\*[A-Z]|\n### |\Z)",
                  resume, re.DOTALL)
    if m:
        block = m.group(1).lower()
        found = [n for n in _TANGENT_ONLY_CLIENTS + _TANGENT_ONLY_MARKERS
                 if n in block]
        if found:
            issues.append({
                "category": "Integrity - Employer Attribution (reverse)",
                "detail": (
                    f"Tangent Apps freelance client work found inside the "
                    f"Prospecta Marketing entry: {', '.join(found)}. Vocation "
                    "Action Network, DNVR/PHNX, Lacroix Hockey / Drill House, "
                    "Ahead of the Curve Media and Honeycomb Strategies are "
                    "Tangent Apps engagements, never Prospecta clients. Move "
                    "this content into the Tangent Apps entry."
                ),
                "severity": "critical",
            })
    cl = cover_letter.lower()
    if "prospecta" in cl:
        for marker in _TANGENT_ONLY_CLIENTS + _TANGENT_ONLY_MARKERS:
            idx = cl.find(marker)
            if idx == -1:
                continue
            window = cl[max(0, idx - 300): idx + 300]
            if "prospecta" in window and "tangent" not in window:
                issues.append({
                    "category": "Integrity - Employer Attribution (reverse)",
                    "detail": (
                        f"Cover letter appears to attribute Tangent Apps work "
                        f"('{marker}') to Prospecta Marketing. These are "
                        "different employers."
                    ),
                    "severity": "critical",
                })
                break
    return issues
```

**Also add to `data/background.md`'s master employer-attribution Calibration
Note**, immediately after the Tangent Apps client list:

> **This list runs in BOTH directions.** Just as no client outside this list may
> appear under Tangent Apps, no client *on* this list may ever be described
> inside the Prospecta Marketing entry, or attributed to Prospecta in a cover
> letter — including by unnamed description ("an organization with a membership
> base in the millions," "a prayer-logging system," "a sports media startup").
> Vocation Action Network's plugin work in particular has repeatedly been placed
> under Prospecta because the WordPress-plugin detail is the strongest available
> engineering evidence; it belongs to Tangent Apps regardless of how useful it
> would be under the W2 employer.

### R3 — HIGH — **prompt/process fix** — The parsed job-title field is the actual ATS-level cause of dev-family silence, and there is one legitimate lever left unused

Follows directly from R1's research. Recruiters filter on the **extracted title
field**, not the resume text. Austin's extracted current-title field reads
`Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive`. A recruiter running a Boolean
filter for `"Software Engineer" OR "Full Stack" OR "Developer"` will never
surface him — regardless of how strong the packet is, and regardless of what the
hiring-manager sim thought.

**The hard integrity line stays exactly where it is.** The Calibration Note
forbidding "Software Engineer" in the Experience title field is correct, it is
not up for debate, and I am not proposing to soften it. (Note: it has been
violated 3 times anyway — `waymark` shipped `*Software Engineer (Full-Stack &
Internal Tooling) - Feb 2024-Present*` with the real title demoted to a
subordinate line, and `neurohire` / `comma-ai` shipped `Senior SEO Specialist &
Web Developer`. **That rule needs a deterministic backstop too** — see R4.)

What *is* available and currently underused:

**(a) The tagline is the one title-shaped string Austin fully controls, and it is
already doing the right thing.** `Software Engineer (Full-Stack) - Denver, CO`
sits directly under the name. Many parsers pick the line under the name up as a
headline/current-title candidate. Keep this. It is the strongest legitimate ATS
title lever in the system and the house style already gets it right.

**(b) Add the location to the contact line as a separate labeled field.** Right
now location lives only in the tagline. `_check_contact_line` doesn't require it
and `candidate_info.md` isn't surfacing it there. Parsers extract `Location:` /
city-state from the contact block; missing it costs geographic filter matches.
Add to `resume_best_practices.md` §Section Structure item 1.3 and
`draft_agent.py`'s header spec:

> The contact line must include location as its own labeled field:
> `Email: ... | Phone: ... | Location: Denver, CO | LinkedIn: ... | GitHub: ...`
> Separate fields with a pipe and spaces so the parser has a delimiter to split
> on rather than guessing from label prefixes alone.

**(c) List core skills as bare tokens in the Core Skills block, not only inside
prose.** Skill extraction is the weakest entity type in every parser (70–90%
accuracy vs. 99%+ for contact fields), and the documented mitigation is to list
skills "once in a dedicated 'Skills' section as bare tokens, once embedded in
bullets" ([Resume Optimizer Pro](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/how-resume-parsers-actually-work)).
The current house style — bold-labeled comma-separated *paragraphs* — is
half-right: the tokens are comma-separated (good), but they are wrapped in
sentence-like connective phrasing ("REST/AJAX API design, MySQL schema design,
session/auth handling, headless-CMS architecture"). Tighten the rule in
`resume_best_practices.md` §Section Structure item 3:

> Within each bold-labeled category, write **bare comma-separated technology
> tokens only** — no verbs, no connective phrases, no parentheticals except an
> acronym gloss. `**Backend:** Node.js, Express, PHP, MySQL, REST API design,
> session and auth handling` is correct. `**Backend:** building REST/AJAX APIs

Violations found in shipped output:

  `**Prospecta Marketing** — *Software Engineer (Full-Stack & Internal Tooling) - Feb 2024-Present*`
  with the real title demoted to `*Official title on record: Senior SEO
  Specialist & Account Executive; scope: ...*`
- `neurohire-software-engineer-2026-07-26`: `*Senior SEO Specialist & Web Developer*`
- `comma-ai-web-software-engineer-2026-07-26`: `*Senior SEO Specialist & Web Developer*`

**Exact fix — `src/structure_lint.py`:**

```python
_OFFICIAL_TITLES = {
    "prospecta marketing": "senior seo specialist & account executive",
}
_TITLE_DRIFT_RE = re.compile(
    r"\b(software engineer|web developer|full[- ]stack developer|"
    r"frontend developer|front[- ]end developer|backend developer|"
    r"developer|engineer)\b",
    re.IGNORECASE,
)

def _check_employer_title_integrity(resume: str) -> list[dict]:
    """The Prospecta title line must match the employer-of-record title
    exactly. This is background.md's single hard integrity boundary (a
    mismatch against employer records reads as fabrication in a reference or
    background check) and it was crossed on 3 real shipped resumes with a
    prompt rule alone."""
    issues = []
    for company, official in _OFFICIAL_TITLES.items():
        m = re.search(
            rf"\*\*{re.escape(company.title())}\*\*[^\n]*\n?[^\n]*",
            resume, re.IGNORECASE,
        )
        if not m:
            continue
        header = m.group(0)
        title_m = re.search(r"\*([^*]+)\*", header)
        if not title_m:
            continue
        title_text = title_m.group(1)
        if title_text.lower().startswith("scope:"):
            continue
        if official.split(" &")[0] in title_text.lower():
            continue
        if _TITLE_DRIFT_RE.search(title_text):
            issues.append({
                "category": "Integrity - Employer Title of Record",
                "detail": (
                    f"The {company.title()} title line reads {title_text!r}, "
                    f"which is not the employer-of-record title "
                    f"({official.title()!r}). Adding 'Engineer'/'Developer' to "
                    "the literal title field is background.md's one hard "
                    "integrity boundary: a title mismatch against employer "
                    "records reads as fabrication in a reference or background "
                    "check. Restore the exact official title and convey the "
                    "real technical scope via the '*Scope: ...*' line, the "
                    "tagline, and the Summary instead."
                ),
                "severity": "critical",
            })
    return issues
```

Also add to `data/resume_best_practices.md` §Integrity Guardrails, verbatim:

>   demote it to a subordinate "official title on record:" line beneath a
>   preferred one — that is the same violation with an extra step.

```python
_TYPOGRAPHIC_FIXES = [
    ("—", ", "),   # em dash
    ("–", "-"),    # en dash
    ("‘", "'"), ("’", "'"),
    ("“", '"'), ("”", '"'),
    ("…", "..."),
]

def _normalize_typography(text: str) -> str:
    """Last-mile, deterministic. The em-dash ban is a prompt rule the model
    has failed on real runs even after a targeted repair pass claimed to
    have fixed it (waymark, 2026-08-11 — 2 em dashes shipped). A string
    substitution should never depend on a model round-trip."""
    for old, new in _TYPOGRAPHIC_FIXES:
        text = text.replace(old, new)
    return re.sub(r"\s+,", ",", re.sub(r",\s*,", ",", text))
```

spec, or it will mangle the separator. With ` | ` as the separator, it is safe.

- **Date format.** `MMM YYYY - MMM YYYY` parses; `2023-2025`, `1/23-3/25`, and

- **DOCX beats PDF.** "DOCX is XML with guaranteed extraction order; PDFs depend

- **Recruiters search the parsed JSON, not the resume.** "That JSON is what the

  [Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/blog/how-recruiters-filter-resumes-using-ats-insider-breakdown)
  and [LinkedIn Recruiter's own Boolean docs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295).
- **Skills extraction is the weakest entity type** (70–90% accuracy vs. 99%+ for
  contact fields); the documented mitigation is bare tokens in a dedicated
  section *plus* embedded mentions in bullets (Resume Optimizer Pro). Drives R3(c).

Where the evidence is **thin**: I found no controlled study quantifying how often
a run-on company/title line actually causes a title-field extraction failure.
The mechanism is documented by multiple vendors and the direction is unanimous,
but the magnitude ("15 percent of resumes" for the title-first case) comes from a
single source. I am ranking R1 first on cost-benefit — it is free to fix and the
downside is zero — not because I can prove the effect size.

### 3.3 Knockout questions — the likely Automattic mechanism

Binary pre-screen filters on the application form (work authorization, licenses,
hard years-of-experience minimums) that "eliminate an application instantly, with
zero human review." An instant or overnight rejection is their signature.
Convergent across [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/knockout-questions-answer-application/),
[jobsearch.guide](https://newsletter.jobsearch.guide/p/knockout-questions),
and [Truffle](https://www.hiretruffle.com/blog/knockout-questions).
Drives R7(a).

### 3.4 The eye-tracking basis for the "Top Third" doctrine — holds up

Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study (30 recruiters, 10 weeks, eye-tracking hardware)
found a 7.4-second average scan, and — the specific finding that matters here —
recruiters fixate on **current title and company first**, then previous title,
then dates, then education
([Ladders study PDF](https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf),
[HR Dive coverage](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/),
[PRNewswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html)).

Worth flagging honestly: the 6-second figure is
[contested as over-cited](https://spectacletalentpartners.com/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth/),
and the study is a vendor study with n=30 and no peer review. The *ordering*
finding (title/company first) replicates across the 2012 and 2018 runs and is the
part this codebase actually depends on, so the doctrine stands. But
`resume_best_practices.md` §The Top Third should soften "eye-tracking research on
resume review consistently finds this" to "the most-cited eye-tracking study on
resume review (Ladders, n=30, 2012 and 2018) finds this" — the current phrasing
overstates the evidence base.

### 3.5 Cover letters — genuinely contested, and the current 400-word investment is defensible

The read-rate data is all over the place: 26% of recruiters read them
consistently; ~50% of hiring managers do; 45% read the letter *before* the
resume; one survey of 10,000 recruiters had 61% saying they don't matter much
([ResumeGenius cover letter statistics](https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics),
[TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/do-hiring-managers-read-cover-letters),
[TheLadders](https://www.theladders.com/career-advice/do-recruiters-read-cover-letters-the-answer-may-surprise-you)).

The one point of agreement is the asymmetry: **49% say a strong letter can secure
an interview; 18% say a weak one can sink an otherwise strong candidate.** Smaller
companies and startups read them more than large enterprises do.

Verdict: the current investment level is right, and the letters this system
produces are genuinely good (see §5). But given the read-rate uncertainty, R1's
resume-parsing fix should be prioritized far above any further cover-letter
tuning.

### 3.6 Referrals — the lever nobody in this system is pulling

Referred candidates get interviews at **4–10x** the rate of cold applicants by
the conservative estimates, and referrals account for ~40% of hires while
representing ~7% of applications
([refer.me — data behind response rates](https://www.refer.me/blog/do-job-referrals-actually-work-data-behind-response-rates),
[CNBC on application channels, Jan 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/12/cold-applying-is-still-the-no-1-way-to-get-a-new-job-but-this-method-is-quickly-getting-more-common.html)).
The high-end figures (40–60% interview rates for referrals) come from
referral-platform vendors and should be discounted; the 4–10x range is the
defensible one.

This is not a code change, but it is the honest answer to "highest-leverage fix
to actually get interviews." Fourteen cold applications at 2–3% is 0.4 expected
callbacks. Fourteen referred applications at even 15% is 2.1. **No achievable
resume improvement moves the number as much as changing the channel does** —
and per §3.1, resume quality cannot even be measured at this sample size.

### 3.7 Breadth vs. depth — verdict

**The v2 audit's fix was correct and should not be reverted. But it addressed the
wrong half of the problem, and the data since 2026-08-06 shows it.**

**What the post-change data says.** The v2 audit removed the platform-name
breadth line (Webflow/Wix/SquareSpace) from Core Skills on engineering JDs.
Comparing the 13 runs before 2026-08-07 to the 20 after:

| | Pre (n=13) | Post (n=20) |
|---|---|---|
| advance verdict | 38% | 60% |
| `narrative_coherence` present | 77% | 90% |
| `narrative_coherence` at medium+ | 46% | 40% |

The advance rate improved meaningfully (confounded by many simultaneous changes).
But `narrative_coherence` did **not** go away — it got *more* frequent and
slightly *less* severe. The generalist objection is still firing on 18 of the
last 20 runs.

**Where it is coming from now.** Reading the post-change concerns verbatim, not
one of them mentions the skills section. Every single one points at the same
thing — the **Tangent Apps umbrella entry**:

- logical: "freelance 'Tangent Apps' engagements (Honeycomb, Vocation Action
  Network, Lacroix Hockey) read as **scattered small-scale side projects**"
- bounteous: "freelance work history ... **taking up significant resume real
  estate that could reinforce core AEO expertise instead**"
- camber / roboflow: "the freelance work is **scattered, low-scale, single-person
  projects**"
- trivelta-ts: "freelance web dev by night **across many small unrelated
  clients** (dental group CMS, sports media site, hockey rink booking tool,
  WordPress plugin)"
- coursera: "**three disparate small projects** (sustainability firm, hockey
  rink, media company)"

Four unrelated verticals — sports media, youth hockey, real estate, religious
nonprofit — in one entry, on a one-page resume, is the breadth signal now. And
`background.md`'s Calibration Note **mandates** two of them onto nearly every
resume:

> "DNVR/PHNX and Lacroix Hockey/Drill House Sports Center should default to
> INCLUDED in the Tangent Apps umbrella entry across nearly all resumes and role
> families, **not selectively cut for JD-relevance the way other freelance
> bullets are.**"

**What the external evidence says, and where it disagrees.**

The convergent finding — across a technical-recruiting firm's reviewer guidance,
a first-person account from a reviewer of 400+ tech resumes, and Monster's
editorial guidance — is that **breadth belongs on the page but must be visibly
subordinate to depth**, and that a flat list of co-equal items is the specific
artifact that destroys the depth signal. Monster's own framing is "specialist,
with breadth," warning that a "hodgepodge resume is more likely to confuse
hiring managers who may think of you as a dabbler without depth."
([Monster](https://www.monster.com/career-advice/article/choosing-your-resume-strategy-hot-jobs),
[400-resume reviewer account](https://medium.com/@emilyhustlenyc/i-have-reviewed-over-400-resumes-for-tech-roles-here-is-what-actually-gets-you-the-phone-screen-d9f5ebbafac0),
[Exodata on technical-resume red flags](https://exodata.io/red-flags-in-technical-resumes-what-hiring-managers-miss/)).

The **company-stage split is real and it cuts in Austin's favor**, but only
partly. The T-shaped literature converges on: "At startups, depth becomes a
slight liability and breadth becomes the asset, because a 30-person company can't
afford a 'distributed systems specialist'" — versus enterprise scale, where
cross-functional interfacing dominates
([Deel on T-shaped roles](https://www.deel.com/deel-works/t-shaped-roles-cross-functional-skills/),
[Founder Institute on hiring T-shaped professionals](https://fi.co/insight/hiring-t-shaped-professionals-for-your-startup),
[LastRound on startup vs. enterprise resumes](https://lastroundai.com/blog/get-hired-at-startup)).
The consistent qualifier across all of them is that breadth is an asset **at the
conversation stage**, not the screening stage — the screen is still run on
"can this person do the specific job," and only then does range become a
differentiator.

**Evidence I am explicitly discounting.** The widely-circulated "repositioning
from generalist to specialist raised interview rate from 1 in 47 to 1 in 12"
figure is, on inspection, an **unpublished internal test on 50 job seekers by a
content-marketing blog**, with no methodology, no controls, and no seniority
breakdown
([The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/the-death-of-the-generalist-resume/)).
It points the same direction as the qualitative sources, which is the only
reason it is mentioned. It should carry no independent weight, and I flag it
because it is the first result for nearly every search on this topic and will
keep resurfacing.

**Verdict, three parts:**

**(1) The current Core Skills rule is correctly calibrated. Keep it verbatim.**
"Express breadth in stacks shipped, not platforms touched," WordPress/PHP always
stays, Webflow/Wix/SquareSpace role-family gated. Nothing in the post-change data
argues against it and the advance rate went up. Do not revisit this.

**(2) The Tangent Apps default-inclusion Calibration Note is now the binding
constraint, and it is too aggressive.** This is the part where I am pushing back
on Austin's stated preference. The note's reasoning is:

> "Their value isn't primarily technical, it's differentiator evidence:
> adaptability, quick thinking under real pressure, and client communication,
> which the candidate considers rare, load-bearing traits."

Austin is **half right, and the data says which half**. Lacroix is cited in the
hiring manager's `positive_signals` on **13 of 32 runs (41%) — the single
most-praised item in the entire corpus.** It fully earns its mandated slot. DNVR
is cited on **6 of 32 (19%)**, while simultaneously being one of the named
components of the "scattered" objection on 5 runs. DNVR is roughly break-even at
best, and it is the one paying the coherence tax.

**(3) The exact replacement.** In `data/background.md` Calibration Notes, replace
the DNVR/Lacroix default-inclusion note with:

> **Freelance entry composition (revised 2026-08-22 audit — supersedes the prior
> "DNVR and Lacroix both default to INCLUDED across nearly all resumes" rule).**
> The Tangent Apps umbrella entry carries a coherence cost that scales with the
> number of *unrelated verticals* visible in it, not with its bullet count: 10
> independent hiring-manager reviews have named the freelance entry's spread
> across sports media, youth hockey, real estate, and nonprofit work as
> "scattered," "disparate," or "diluting." Cap it at **three client bullets
> maximum on any resume**, chosen as follows.
>
> - **Lacroix Hockey / Drill House Sports Center is a hard floor and always
>   appears.** It is cited as a standout positive by 41% of independent
>   hiring-manager reviews, the highest of any single item in this document. Its
>   95% cost-reduction figure is the strongest quantified business outcome
>   available. Never cut it, never compress it below one full line.
> - **The other two slots go to whichever clients are genuinely closest to the
>   JD's stack or domain.** Ahead of the Curve Media on any JD wanting
>   React/Node/payments; Vocation Action Network on any JD wanting PHP/MySQL,
>   plugin/CMS architecture, or security work; Honeycomb Strategies on SEO and
>   marketing JDs.
> - **DNVR/PHNX is no longer a default.** Include it when the JD wants
>   crisis/ambiguity/ownership signal, when it is genuinely the closest stack
>   match (WordPress frontend builds), or when a slot is otherwise unfilled.
>   Omit it when three stronger-matched clients exist. Its adaptability story is
>   real and it is cited positively on 19% of reviews, but it is also one of the
>   named components of the "scattered small projects" objection on 5 reviews,
>   and it carries no dollar or percentage figure to offset that. When it is
>   included, both required pieces still apply (the WordPress frontend
>   design/build AND the 48-hour crisis) — that rule is unchanged.
> - **What replaces the lost breadth signal:** the breadth claim is carried by
>   the Core Skills category labels (three production stacks) and by the
>   Prospecta entry's own range, not by client count in the freelance entry.
>   Four clients does not read as "more versatile" than three; it reads as
>   "smaller pieces."

And update `structure_lint._check_differentiator_defaults` to match: require
Lacroix at `medium` severity (raised from `low`), and drop the DNVR requirement
entirely rather than flagging its absence.

**One thing this verdict does not claim.** I cannot show from this repository
that the breadth calibration affects real-world callbacks, because with 14
submissions there is no callback signal to correlate against (§0.2). Everything
above is calibrated against the hiring-manager simulation plus external evidence.
That is the best available basis, and it is a real one — but it is a proxy, and
it should be labeled as such rather than treated as validated.

## 4. Where I disagree

**4.1 — With the framing of the question itself.** "39 applications, 0 callbacks"
is 14 applications, 0 callbacks, and that is the most likely single outcome at
industry base rates (§0). I am putting this first because every other
disagreement follows from it: this system has been tuned hard against a signal
that does not exist yet. Nine finished packets are sitting unsubmitted. Ship
those before changing anything else.

**4.2 — With `config.py`'s justification for `FIT_GATE_MIN_SCORE = 55`.** The
comment says raising it to 70 "made the gate fire on nearly every run (every
historical run scored 62-78%), and a gate that always fires just trains reflexive
'y' answers." The mechanism is real; the conclusion inverts it. A gate that fires
on nearly every run against a target list where 6 of 14 submissions were to
provably unmeetable reqs is **reporting a true fact about the target list.** The
fix is a better target list plus a hard block on years-shortfalls (R6), not a
lower threshold. Lowering it converted a true positive into silence.

**4.3 — With `resume_best_practices.md`'s one-line Experience entry format.** The
file justifies it as "this one-line format replaces the old two-line
company/title layout to save vertical space." That trade is backwards: vertical
space is a solved problem in this pipeline (`length_fit.py` renders and counts
real pages, and current resumes fit with room), while parseability is
load-bearing and currently broken (§2 R1). Revert to two lines.

**4.4 — With `classifier.py`'s docstring claim that "there's no cost to leaning
toward 'hybrid'."** True when written, false since 2026-08-19, and the Roboflow
run is the receipt (§2 R10). Stale comments that assert safety are worse than no
comment.

**4.5 — With `resume_best_practices.md`'s characterization of the eye-tracking
evidence.** "eye-tracking research on resume review consistently finds this"
overstates what is one vendor study with n=30, run twice, with the headline
figure independently contested. The ordering finding is sound and the doctrine
should stay; the citation language should be honest about its base (§3.4).

**4.6 — Partial disagreement with Austin's DNVR/Lacroix default-inclusion
preference.** Detailed in §3.7(2)–(3). Lacroix is validated by the data and
should be strengthened, not weakened. DNVR should stop being a mandated default.
I want to be explicit that I am disagreeing with a stated preference here and
that the evidence is a proxy (hiring-manager simulation), not real callbacks.

**4.7 — Where I do NOT disagree, despite looking hard.** The no-fabrication line,
the employer-of-record title boundary, the Kharon containment override, the dental
anonymization rule, the Tangent-Apps-names-stay-real correction, the
gap-confession ban, the `jd_coverage` vs `draft_defect` split, and the decision to
deny the hiring-manager sim access to `background.md` are all correct and should
not be touched. The 2026-08-19 degree-line reversal (bare year → "Completed March
2026") is also correct, and the earlier v2 recommendation it overturned was
wrong — good self-correction.

## 5. What's already working well — do not break these

1. **`.docx`-only export.** Correct per every parsing source; DOCX has guaranteed
   extraction order, PDF does not. Do not add a PDF path for submissions.
2. **The metadata fix in `docx_export._set_core_properties`.** Verified against a
   real shipped file: `docProps/core.xml` now carries the real author and current
   timestamps, with none of python-docx's 2013 default fingerprint. Clean.
3. **Bullet rendering.** Verified `word/numbering.xml` is present and the
   `ListBullet` style carries a real `numPr`/`numId`. Bullets are genuine list
   items, not indented text. Fine.
4. **Single-column, no tables, no images, no headers/footers, standard section
   headers.** Every parsing source's core requirement, satisfied.
5. **The deterministic-backstop philosophy in `structure_lint.py` /
   `style_lint.py`.** This is the best thing about the codebase. Every check has
   a comment naming the real shipped bug it exists to prevent and why a prompt
   rule was insufficient. Three of my top recommendations are just "apply this
   same philosophy to three more rules that don't have it yet."
6. **`quality_key`'s severity weighting and best-draft-across-iterations
   tracking.** Genuinely subtle and correct — a fabricating draft can no longer
   tie a merely-long draft.
7. **`jd_coverage` vs `draft_defect` classification, and never feeding
   `jd_coverage` to `revise()`.** This is what stops the model from writing
   gap-confession sentences. It works.
8. **Denying the hiring-manager sim access to `background.md`.** The whole reason
   its concerns are useful. Never give it ground truth.
9. **`_reroute_leaked_positive_signals` / `_reroute_unformatted_positives`.**
   Unglamorous output-shape recovery with real documented failure cases behind
   each one.
10. **The cover letters are genuinely good.** Reading the runpod, waymark, and
    camber letters end to end: specific, company-anchored, no template smell, real
    anecdotes, no groveling beyond the one closing tic in R12(c). Whatever the
    problem is, it is not letter quality.
11. **`data/background.md` itself.** It is the most valuable asset in this
    repository by a wide margin — the calibration notes encode dozens of real,
    hard-won corrections, and the "never adopted this alternate figure and here is
    why" entries are exactly right.
