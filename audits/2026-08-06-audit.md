# 1. Recurring rejection patterns

## Pattern A — `seniority_calibration`: "the engineering work is a side function of a marketing job"
**11 concerns across 9 of 9 hiring-manager reviews. This is the single most frequent rejection driver.**

- **comma.ai** — *"[high] seniority_calibration: Candidate is currently a 'Senior SEO Specialist & Web Developer' whose primary job is SEO/account management, not full-time software engineering."*
- **Hims & Hers** — *"[high] seniority_calibration: Title is 'Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive' as the primary/current role — the actual software engineering work is either freelance side projects or recent, scoped plugin work, not 5 years of full-time production engineering ownership at scale."*
- **Clerkie (08-05)** — *"[medium] seniority_calibration: no full-time 'software engineer' job title on the resume at all — all engineering claims come from a marketing role and freelance/consulting side projects, so the depth of sustained team-based engineering practice (code review, CI/CD, on-call, working within an eng team) is unproven."*
- **USA Home Listings** — *"the fullstack engineering work described (CTO bug-fix assistance, plugin building) is a side/secondary responsibility within a marketing job."*
- **Trivelta** — *"[high] seniority_calibration: Title and summary claim 'Senior' frontend capability, but the flagship proof point for React/TypeScript/Next.js is a timeboxed take-home exercise."*
- **Fivetran** — *"he's 2 years into his career managing local dental clients... a fundamentally different scale, stakeholder complexity, and content sophistication."*
- **SigParser** (the inverse) — *"[medium] seniority_calibration: ...this reads more like a mid-level engineer than someone applying for a Junior/entry-level role."*

Note the SigParser inversion: the system oversold on a junior req while underselling on senior reqs.
There is no calibration step that reads the JD's seniority band and sets the register accordingly.

### R1. Split `gaps` into "fixable draft defects" vs "JD coverage gaps" — and never feed the latter to `revise()`
**[PROCESS] — highest expected impact. This one change explains the 78-point plateau AND the gap-confession pattern.**

**The problem.** `score_agent`'s `gaps` array conflates two incompatible things:
- *"the Prospecta entry is missing an AE bullet"* — a draft defect, fixable.
- *"JD explicitly requires 'Experience building backend APIs with Python'... this is a material missing qualification"* — a candidate capability gap, **unfixable by any draft**.

Both get handed to `draft_agent.revise()` with the instruction *"Revise the resume and cover letter to
address ONLY the items above."* Faced with an unfixable gap, the model's only remaining move is to
write about it. That is exactly what happened, four times, and it is the direct cause of Pattern E.
The revision prompt already tries to forbid this in prose; the structural pressure wins.

It also caps the score permanently. Six runs plateaued at 78 with the identical composition: two or
three real draft defects plus three or four "JD Requirement Coverage" gaps that no iteration can
retire. 85 is unreachable for any JD with a genuine gap, which is nearly all of them.

**Change 1 — `src/score_agent.py`, `SCORE_TOOL` input schema.** Add a required `kind` enum to each gap:

```python
"kind": {
    "type": "string",
    "enum": ["draft_defect", "jd_coverage"],
    "description": (
        "draft_defect = something a revision of these documents can actually fix "
        "(structure, ordering, vague phrasing, a real background-doc bullet that "
        "should have been surfaced, a house-style violation, a stale fact). "
        "jd_coverage = the JD asks for experience the candidate genuinely does not "
        "have anywhere in the background document. A jd_coverage gap is INFORMATIONAL "
        "ONLY — it reports a real limitation to the candidate, it is not a defect in "
        "the draft and no rewrite can close it."
    ),
},
```

**Change 2 — `src/score_agent.py`, `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS`.** Append:

> Classify every gap as `draft_defect` or `jd_coverage`. Be honest about the split: if the background
> document contains no support for a JD requirement, that is `jd_coverage`, not a draft defect, and
> you must NOT phrase it as something the writer should "address," "acknowledge," or "surface" — the
> correct handling of a `jd_coverage` gap is silence in the documents. Score the draft on how well it
> presents the candidate's *actual* material against this JD, not on how much of the JD the candidate
> covers. A draft that leads perfectly with everything genuinely available should be able to reach
> 90+ even when the candidate is missing several JD requirements; do not deduct for `jd_coverage`
> gaps at all. Deduct only for `draft_defect` items, weak or missing quantification of claims the
> background doc *does* support, ATS/keyword problems, house-style violations, and generic voice.

**Change 3 — `src/supervisor.py`, line 62.** Pass only fixable items into the revision:

```python
fixable = [g for g in result.gaps if g.get("kind") != "jd_coverage"]
draft = draft_agent.revise(..., fixable + lint_issues, result.integrity_flags)
```

**Change 4 — `src/supervisor.py`, loop condition and status.** Base both on fixable gaps:

```python
while (
    result.score < config.SCORE_THRESHOLD or result.integrity_flags or lint_issues
) and iteration < config.MAX_ITERATIONS:
```
becomes
```python
fixable = [g for g in result.gaps if g.get("kind") != "jd_coverage"]
while (
    (result.score < config.SCORE_THRESHOLD or result.integrity_flags or lint_issues or fixable)
    and iteration < config.MAX_ITERATIONS
):
```

**Change 5 — `src/report.py`.** Render the two lists under separate headings: "Fixable issues
remaining" and "Real gaps between your background and this JD (nothing in the draft can close these —
worth knowing before the interview)." This makes the report considerably more useful to you as well.

### R2. Keep the best draft, not the last one
**[PROCESS] — second-highest impact; pure bug, no prompt work.**

`supervisor.run()` returns whatever the final iteration produced. In **5 of 10 runs the shipped
document was measurably worse than one the system had already generated**:

| Run | Iteration scores (score / gaps / flags) | Shipped |
|---|---|---|
| SigParser | 72/4/1 → **78/0/0** → 72/4/2 | the 72 |
| Clerkie (08-05) | 82/4/1 → 82/4/1 → 72/7/1 | the 72 |
| Unknown/trading | **82/4/0** → 78/9/2 → 72/8/4 | the 72 |
| Fivetran | **78/6/0** → 72/6/1 → 72/5/2 | the 72 |
| USA Home Listings | 78/5/2 → **82/4/1** → 78/4/1 | the 78 |

The SigParser case is the clearest: iteration 2 produced a draft with **zero gaps and zero integrity
flags**, and the system threw it away.

**Fix — `src/supervisor.py`.** Track and return the best-scoring draft:

```python
best = (draft, result, lint_issues, iteration)

def _quality_key(res, lint):
    # fewer blockers first, then higher score
    return (-(len(res.integrity_flags) + len(lint)), res.score)

# after each scoring pass:
if _quality_key(result, lint_issues) > _quality_key(best[1], best[2]):
    best = (draft, result, lint_issues, iteration)
```
Return `best` rather than the loop's terminal state, and add the winning iteration number to
`SupervisorResult` so the report can say "shipped iteration 2 of 3."

### R3. Stop revising when there is nothing actionable to revise
**[PROCESS]**

SigParser iteration 2 came back with score 78, **0 gaps, 0 integrity flags, 0 style issues**. The loop
continued solely because `78 < 85`. `revise()` was then called with `gaps_text = "(none)"` and
`flags_text = "(none)"`, alongside the instruction *"Revise the resume and cover letter to address
ONLY the items above."* Nothing was above. The model's own note for that pass:

> *"[iteration 3] No gaps or integrity flags were listed for this pass, so the resume and cover letter are carried forward unchanged."*

It said it carried them forward unchanged; the score dropped from 78 to 72 with 4 new gaps and 2 new
integrity flags. Free-running revision with no target actively degrades output.

**Fix — `src/supervisor.py`, before the `while` body:**

```python
if not fixable and not result.integrity_flags and not lint_issues:
    # Nothing actionable left. Score is capped by real JD coverage gaps,
    # not by anything a rewrite can change. Stop here rather than letting
    # the drafter free-run.
    break
```
(With R1 applied, this is the natural terminal condition.)

### R5. Add a deterministic structural lint for the rules the LLM keeps missing
**[PROCESS] — retires Patterns F and G, both of which currently cost a full iteration each.**

New file `src/structure_lint.py`, run wherever `style_lint` runs. All checks are regex/string-level
and free. Proposed checks:

1. **Prospecta floor** — locate the Prospecta entry (the block between the `**Prospecta Marketing**`
   line and the next `**`-bolded company line); confirm ≥3 bullets; confirm the first bullet matches
   an SEO vocabulary set (`SEO|keyword|visibility|schema|crawl|AEO|technical audit|conversion path`)
   and the second matches an account/client vocabulary set (`client|account|stakeholder|conference|
   funnel|agency relationship|point of contact`). Emit a `high` severity issue naming the missing
   discipline. This is the exact check `score_agent` is asked to do in prose and has missed on the
   first pass 5 times.
2. **Stale credential language** — flag any of `currently completing|working toward (a|my)? ?BS|
   pursuing a (BS|Bachelor)|in progress` appearing within 200 chars of `Computer Science`, when
   `background.md` says "graduated." Also flag `(20\d\d)` degree years that are in the past paired
   with present-tense verbs.
3. **Heading contract** — exactly one `^## ` line; every other section header is `^### `; the four
   expected headers (`Summary`, `Core Skills`, `Professional Experience`, `Education & Certifications`)
   all present and spelled exactly.
4. **Contact line** — one single line containing `Email:` and at least one of `Phone:`/`LinkedIn:`.
   The Unknown/trading run shipped a split header; the score agent caught it as `low` and it never
   got fixed.
5. **Kharon containment** — flag if `Kharon` appears anywhere in `resume.md` at all.
6. **Cover letter word count** — flag over 350 words (house style already says this; nothing enforces
   it). Measured: 7 of 10 shipped letters exceeded it; comma.ai shipped **577 words**, 65% over, and
   its HM specifically noted density.
7. **Resume length pre-check** — flag over ~520 words before rendering. Empirically the two runs that
   failed one-page enforcement were 582 and 587 words; the largest successful one-page resume was 544.
   Catching this at draft time avoids up to three Sonnet `shorten()` calls per run.

### R7. Fix the hiring-manager concern parser — it is silently discarding severity and category
**[PROCESS] — cheap, and it is currently degrading every HM-driven revision.**

Two defects in `src/hiring_manager.py`:

**(a) Multiple concerns per array element.** The Hims & Hers run returned all six concerns as one
string with embedded newlines. `_CONCERN_RE.match()` parses only the first; the remaining five became
part of `detail` under the first concern's category. Visible verbatim in that report — concerns 2–6
render as unbulleted continuation lines.

**(b) Alternate format not matched.** The Unknown/trading run emitted `medium[seniority_calibration]:`
instead of `[medium] seniority_calibration:`. `_CONCERN_RE` does not match that shape, so **all six
concerns fell through to `severity="medium", category="other"`** and were fed back to `revise()` with
their entire priority signal erased — including two `medium` red flags and a high-value seniority
concern.

**Fix — `src/hiring_manager.py`:**

```python
_CONCERN_RE = re.compile(
    r"^\s*(?:"
    r"\[(?P<sev1>low|medium|high)\]\s*(?P<cat1>[a-z_]+)"      # [high] category:
    r"|(?P<sev2>low|medium|high)\s*\[(?P<cat2>[a-z_]+)\]"      # high[category]:
    r"|(?P<cat3>[a-z_]+)\s*\((?P<sev3>low|medium|high)\)"      # category (high):
    r")\s*:\s*(?P<detail>.+)$",
    re.IGNORECASE,
)
```
and in `as_gap_dicts()`, split each element on newlines first:

```python
for raw in self.concerns:
    for line in (l for l in raw.splitlines() if l.strip()):
        ...
```

Also worth doing: sort the resulting gaps `high → medium → low` before passing to `revise()`, so the
targeted-revision budget goes to the concerns that actually drove the reject.

### R8. Surface the production-engineering evidence that is sitting unused in `background.md`
**[PROCESS] — this is the direct counter to Patterns A, C, and D, and it requires zero new facts.**

Playing offense here is entirely legitimate: every item below is already in `background.md`, verbatim,
and is simply not being selected.

**(a) The Basecamp AI Agent is the strongest production-software artifact in the document and it is
being actively suppressed by its own Calibration Note.**

`background.md` describes it as: *"Built a serverless AI agent in Python on AWS (Lambda, DynamoDB, S3)...
DynamoDB used for state management across the pipeline; wrote all the code myself... Live in production,
used daily for real Prospecta work — not a tutorial or demo project."*

This is: production Python, production AWS serverless, production LLM integration, sole authorship,
daily real use, **at the W2 employer** — not freelance, not a side client, not a take-home. It is the
single best available answer to *"[high] red_flag: No evidence of building systems other engineering
teams depend on"* and to *"all engineering experience is either a side-function role or freelance."*

It appeared as a real bullet in **one** run (Trivelta) plus the general Indeed resume. It did not
appear in Clerkie (08-05), Hims & Hers, SigParser, USA Home Listings, or Unknown/trading — all
dev-family JDs, all rejected or flagged on exactly the objection this bullet answers. Most striking:
the **comma.ai** fit gate listed *"Experience building backend APIs with Python"* as the single missing
requirement, and the run then spent two iterations writing a Python confession into the cover letter.

The cause is this Calibration Note:
> *"Default treatment: a brief mention folded into the Prospecta AEO/AIO or AI-strategy bullet... rather than its own separate bullet or line item — only give it a full standalone bullet when a JD is specifically Python/AWS/AI-agent-relevant enough to warrant the extra emphasis."*

### R9. Give the drafter the seniority band, the company name, and the known-unmeetable requirements
**[PROCESS] — three signals the pipeline already computes and then discards.**

`fit_gate.run()` returns `missing_requirements`, `background_gaps`, and `company_name`. `main.py`
prints them, hands `company_name` to `hiring_gate`, and passes **none of them to `draft_agent.generate()`**.
Consequences observed:

- The drafter rediscovers the missing requirements the hard way, through the score agent, then confesses
  them (Pattern E). Telling it up front — *and telling it what to do about them* — is strictly better.
- **Trivelta** — *"[medium] red_flag: No mention of Trivelta specifically... reads as adaptable to nearly
  any senior frontend posting, not written for this company's mission or product."* The company name was
  extracted successfully and never given to the writer.
- Nothing tells the drafter what seniority band to write in. **SigParser** oversold on a junior req
  (*"raises retention/salary-fit questions for an $85-100k junior slot"*); five senior reqs undersold.

**Fix — `src/draft_agent.py`, `generate()` signature and user message:**

```python
def generate(job_title, jd_text, background_subset, best_practices,
             company_name="", known_missing=None, seniority_band=""):
    ...
    user_message = (
        f"Job title: {job_title}\n"
        f"Hiring company: {company_name or '(not stated in the posting)'}\n"
        f"Target seniority band: {seniority_band or '(infer from the posting)'}\n\n"
        f"Job description:\n{jd_text}\n\n"
        "A pre-screen already determined the background document has NO support for "
        "these JD requirements:\n" + (missing_text or "(none)") + "\n"
        "Do not address, acknowledge, hedge about, or allude to any item on that list "
        "anywhere in either document. Treat them as out of scope and spend the space "
        "on the candidate's strongest genuinely-supported material instead.\n\n"
        "Write a tailored resume and cover letter..."
    )
```

Add `seniority_band` to `FIT_GATE_TOOL`'s schema (`enum: ["junior", "mid", "senior", "lead/staff", "unclear"]`)
— it is one extra field on a Haiku call you are already making.

And add to `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS`:

> Name the hiring company at least once in the cover letter, and once more in a sentence that could
> only have been written for this specific posting (a phrase from the posting's own description of the
> problem, product, or team). A letter that would work unchanged for another company at the same
> seniority reads as a template regardless of how well written it is.
>
> Match the seniority register to the target band. For a junior/entry req, do not lead with "Senior"
> in the tagline or foreground succession/ownership scope — it raises retention and salary-fit
> questions that get candidates screened out as overqualified. For a senior/lead req, lead with the
> largest genuinely-supported scope, ownership, and adoption evidence available, and do not open with
> the tenure framing.

### R11. Rebalance quantification toward the engineering half of the story
**[PROCESS] — targets Pattern C.**

`resume_best_practices.md` says "Quantify wherever the background doc has a number." The background
doc's numbers are overwhelmingly SEO-side, so dev-family resumes come out at 11–14 numeric tokens,
several of them version numbers. Add to §Bullet Writing:

> **Non-metric quantification.** When a bullet has no percentage or dollar figure available, it can
> still be sized. Prefer, in order: the count of things affected (clients, users, members, accounts,
> pages, endpoints, lines rewritten); the count of parties coordinated (teams, agencies, stakeholders);
> the duration or deadline pressure (a 48-hour window, a 10-month engagement); and the adoption fact
> (reused companywide, in daily production use, still maintained). All four of these are real numbers

> A bullet with none of them reads as an unverifiable adjective and gets silently discounted.
>
> **Version numbers do not count as quantification.** "React 18", "Express 4", "React Router v6" are
> ATS keyword tokens, not impact. Do not let them occupy the slot where a scale number belongs.

### R12. ATS additions to `resume_best_practices.md`
**[PROCESS]**

Current §ATS Formatting is solid on structure and dates but says nothing about keyword *placement* or
*frequency*, which is what modern parsers (Greenhouse, Ashby, Workday) actually weight. Add:

> - **Placement beats frequency.** A keyword carries the most weight in the Summary and inside an
>   experience bullet that proves it, and much less in a bare skills list. For each of the JD's top
>   5–8 must-have terms that the background genuinely supports, aim to place it in *two* of three
>   locations: the Core Skills block, the tagline or Summary, and the bullet that demonstrates it.
> - **2–3 appearances, different contexts.** Repeating a primary keyword two or three times in
>   genuinely different sentences reads as depth; repeating it identically reads as stuffing, which
>   current parsers penalize rather than reward.
> - **Mirror the JD's exact string, then gloss it.** If the JD writes "Google Analytics 4," write
>   "Google Analytics 4 (GA4)" rather than "GA4" alone. Literal-match screening still runs before any
>   semantic layer in most stacks. This extends the existing acronym rule in both directions.
> - **Never list a technology with no supporting evidence anywhere in the background doc,** even if it
>   appears in the `[TECH-STACK]` section. A skills-list entry the candidate cannot defend in a
>   technical screen is worth less than the keyword match it buys.

That last rule has already fired twice on the same item, from two different runs:
- NeuroHire — *"[medium] skill_overstated: Core Skills lists 'Docker' but the background doc shows no actual Docker production use... it's padding that can't be defended in an interview."*
- comma.ai iteration 3 — *"Removed the unsupported 'Docker' claim from Core Skills... since it doesn't appear anywhere in the background doc."*

Docker **does** appear — in the `[TECH-STACK]` line, with no supporting work anywhere. Same problem
applies to Java and Swift, both listed as languages with zero supporting bullets; the NeuroHire resume
shipped both in Core Skills.

**Recommended `background.md` edit — new Calibration Note:**
> *"The `[TECH-STACK]` section is an index of things touched, not a claims list. Docker, Git, Java,
> Swift, MongoDB, and Screaming Frog currently appear there with no supporting work described anywhere
> else in this document. Any of these may be listed in Core Skills only when the JD names it AND a real
> bullet elsewhere in this doc demonstrates it; otherwise omit. (Austin: if real Docker, Java, or Swift
> work exists, adding two or three sentences of it to the relevant section would convert three dead
> keywords into live ones.)"*

### R13. Cover letter: hard length cap, and a reframe template to replace the confession reflex
**[PROCESS]**

House style says "under 350 words." Seven of ten shipped letters exceeded it (293 / 353 / 385 / 400 /
403 / 439 / 450 / 484 / 504 / **577**). Two HMs flagged density directly — Clerkie (08-05): *"the three
middle paragraphs are dense, multi-project recaps heavy on implementation detail... it reads as
resume-as-prose"*; Trivelta: *"The second paragraph runs long (5 sentences, ~140 words) and loses focus
by stacking too many examples."* Enforce it deterministically (R5.6).

Second, the drafter needs a *positive* move to make where it currently reaches for a confession. Add to
`data/resume_best_practices.md` §Cover Letter, after the existing "Never volunteer a missing
qualification" block:

> **The adjacent-strength pivot.** Silence is the floor, not the ceiling. When a JD leans on something
> the background doc doesn't support, the strongest available move is usually not silence but a
> paragraph that (1) names the underlying *problem* the requirement exists to solve, in the JD's own
> words; (2) gives one specific, real instance of solving that class of problem with different tools;
> and (3) stops. Never name the missing tool, never characterize your level with it, never promise to
> pick it up.
>
> - Weak (shipped, rejected): *"I haven't worked hands-on in Conductor or Profound specifically; my
>   tracked-analytics background is GA4, Search Console, SEMrush, Ahrefs, and SEO PowerSuite, and I'd
>   expect to pick up a new platform quickly given that overlap."*
> - Strong: *"The part of this role I'd want to talk about is turning visibility data into a decision
>   someone acts on. When one account's bounce rate moved 53.4% to 35.0%, the useful output wasn't the
>   number — it was tracing it to a UX lever rather than a keyword gap and redirecting the next
>   month's priorities on that basis."*
>
> Both are honest. The first tells the reader what you cannot do and then asks them to discount it;
> the second tells them what you do and lets the tooling question come up in the screen, where you can
> answer it in conversation. A reader who is unsure whether you are qualified will resolve that
> uncertainty against you — do not hand them the doubt in writing.

### R14. Don't spend a Sonnet revision on hiring-manager concerns nothing can fix
**[PROCESS]**

`hiring_gate.run()` feeds every rejected review's concerns straight into `revise()`. But the concerns
driving the rejects are overwhelmingly structural facts about the candidate's history —
*"no full-time software engineer job title on the resume at all," "not 5 years of full-time production
engineering ownership," "he's 2 years into his career."* No revision addresses those. The Sonnet call
is spent, and per R2's data the redraft usually makes things worse.

**Fix.** Add a required `fixable` boolean to each concern in `HM_TOOL`'s schema:

```python
"description": (
    "Each concern as ONE self-contained string in the exact format "
    "'[severity] category: detail (fixable|structural)' — append '(fixable)' if a "
    "rewrite of these two documents could plausibly resolve it (weak positioning, "
    "buried evidence, vague bullet, generic letter, wrong emphasis), or '(structural)' "
    "if it is a fact about the candidate's actual history that no rewrite changes "
    "(years of experience, job titles held, technologies never used)."
),
```
Then in `hiring_gate.run()`, skip the revision when every concern is `structural`, return
`passed=False` immediately with a clear status, and surface those concerns in the report under
"Why this application will not clear a hiring-manager bar, regardless of drafting." That is honest,
actionable (it tells you to pick different reqs), and saves a Sonnet call plus a length-fit round trip
per hopeless run.

# 3. External research notes

**The 6-second scan is a rejection timer, not a reading time — and titles dominate it.** The Ladders
eye-tracking studies (6 seconds in 2012, 7.4 seconds in the 2018 update) found reviewers fixate first
on current title and company, then previous title, then dates, then education. Two consequences fed
into R10: the tagline is the highest-leverage string on the page and must match the JD's own title
verbatim where truthful; and an in-progress credential visible in the top third is disproportionately
costly, which is exactly what two hiring managers in this log independently flagged.
Sources: [The Ladders eye-tracking study (2018 PDF)](https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf) ·
[The Ladders — "You have 7.4 seconds"](https://www.theladders.com/career-advice/you-only-get-6-seconds-of-fame-make-it-count) ·
[The Interview Guys — the scan was a rejection time](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/the-6-second-resume-scan-was-never-a-reading-time-it-was-a-rejection/)

**ATS keyword handling in 2026 rewards placement and context, not frequency.** Current-generation
parsers (Workday, Greenhouse, iCIMS, Ashby) apply contextual and semantic matching; the recommended
pattern is placing each priority term in the skills block, the title/summary line, *and* the bullet
that proves it, with each primary keyword appearing 2–3 times in genuinely different contexts. Simple
repetition is now penalized rather than rewarded, and exact-string matching still runs first ("Google
Analytics 4," not "GA4"). Several sources also note that undated or ambiguously dated entries draw
auto-rejection in some configurations — the existing date-precision rules in this repo are well
positioned against that. This informed R12.
Sources: [My Personal Recruiter — what ATS actually look for in 2026](https://mypersonalrecruiter.com/ats-resume-what-applicant-tracking-systems-actually-look-for-in-2026/) ·
[PassTheScan — ATS resume keywords 2026](https://www.passthescan.com/blog/ats-resume-keywords-2026-complete-guide) ·
[OwlApply — ATS-friendly resume guide 2026](https://owlapply.com/en/blog/ats-friendly-resume-guide-2026-format-keywords-score-and-fixes)

**Quantified achievements materially outperform duty statements.** Reported effects cluster around
40% more callbacks for resumes with measurable results versus without, and roughly 2.5x more interview
invitations for quantified profiles versus duty-based ones. Treat the exact percentages as directional
vendor data rather than peer-reviewed findings, but the direction is consistent and large enough to
act on — and it is the basis for R11's "non-metric quantification" rule, which exists precisely
because this candidate's engineering bullets have real countable facts available (members, clients,
teams, lines, adoption) that are currently going unstated.
Sources: [Resumly — why quantifiable results matter](https://www.resumly.ai/blog/importance-of-quantifiable-results-in-resume-bullets) ·
[Resume Optimizer Pro — quantifying achievements](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/including-quantifiable-achievements-in-your-resume)

**Cover letters: tailoring is the whole effect, and length is short.** A field study of 7,000+
applications found a role-tailored letter pulled a 16.4% callback rate versus generic or none; 49% of
hiring managers say a strong letter can secure an interview while 18% say a weak one sinks an
otherwise strong candidate. Recommended shape is a concrete hook, one or two proven results, a clear
ask, at roughly half a page — readable in under a minute, against under 30 seconds of actual attention
for letters that do get opened. This confirms the existing 350-word rule and justifies enforcing it
(R5.6, R13). The same sources note that AI-default register — "I am excited," "I am confident," "I am
passionate," balanced clauses — triggers the same skim-and-skip as a template, which validates the
existing banned-opener list in `resume_best_practices.md`.
Sources: [Resume Genius — 50+ cover letter statistics 2026](https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics) ·
[MetaIntro — cover letters that get read in 2026](https://www.metaintro.com/blog/cover-letter-that-actually-gets-read-2026)

**On admitting gaps.** The 2026 literature is broadly permissive about *employment* gaps — 91% of
hiring managers open to career breaks, gaps of 6–18 months a non-concern for 76% — but the operative
finding transfers cleanly to *skill* gaps and cuts against what this pipeline has been doing:
**"if you sound unsure about whether you're qualified, the hiring manager will be too."** The guidance
where a gap must be addressed is one to two sentences, placed after the pitch and never leading. The
shipped Fivetran, Hims & Hers, and Unknown/trading letters all violate that placement rule, and the
Fivetran HM's *"functions almost as a pre-emptive rejection letter"* is precisely the predicted
failure. This is the evidence base for R6 and R13.
Sources: [Scale.jobs — resume gaps 2026](https://scale.jobs/blog/resume-gaps-how-to-explain-without-getting-rejected) ·
[AiApply — explaining a career gap in a cover letter](https://aiapply.co/blog/how-to-explain-a-career-gap-in-a-cover-letter)

**On job titles and verification.** Consistent guidance: use the official title wherever the field
feeds verification (application forms, background-check inputs), because a mismatch becomes a
discrepancy on the report; reframe in the self-description surfaces instead. This is exactly what the
existing Calibration Note already mandates, independently arrived at. It is one of the strongest
design decisions in this repo and should not be softened.
Sources: [Hiration — changing job title on a resume: reframing vs. lying](https://www.hiration.com/blog/change-job-title-on-resume/) ·
[AiApply — how to change a misleading job title](https://aiapply.co/blog/changing-job-title-on-resume)

# 4. What's already working well — do not break these
