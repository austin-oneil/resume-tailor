## 2. Prioritized recommendations

### 2.1 — CRITICAL — `_check_stale_credentials` is a permanent, unsatisfiable blocker

**File:** `src/structure_lint.py:71-88` | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

I ran the current lint against seven real past resumes. This check fired on
**all seven**, every time on the same thing, and never once on an actual stale
credential.

The cause: the check looks for "in progress" and then searches a 200-character
*backward* window for "computer science". In the mandatory Education &
Certifications block, these two lines are always adjacent:

```
Bachelor of Science, Computer Science, Western Governors University (completed March 2026)
Certifications: HIPAA Certified, ... AWS Solutions Architect - Associate (in progress)
```

The AWS cert's legitimate, truthful "(in progress)" lands inside the window of
the CS degree. There is no legal draft that escapes this: the Education section
is required by `_check_heading_contract`, the AWS cert genuinely is in progress,
and dropping "(in progress)" would be a fabrication.

Because `main.py:76-86` requires `not final_style_issues` for `accepted`, **every
future run is now hard-pinned to `manual_review` regardless of quality.**

**Fix — scope the match to the degree line only:**

```python
_STALE_CREDENTIAL_RE = re.compile(
    r"(currently completing|working toward (a|my)? ?BS|pursuing a (BS|Bachelor)|"
    r"in progress|in-progress|expected \d{4}|anticipated)",
    re.IGNORECASE,
)

def _check_stale_credentials(resume: str, background_subset: str) -> list[dict]:
    if "graduated" not in background_subset.lower():
        return []
    for line in resume.splitlines():
        # Only the line that actually names the degree; a certification line
        # legitimately carries "(in progress)" and must not trip this.
        if not re.search(r"bachelor|b\.?s\.?,|computer science", line, re.IGNORECASE):
            continue
        if re.search(r"certification", line, re.IGNORECASE):
            continue
        m = _STALE_CREDENTIAL_RE.search(line)
        if m:
            return [{...}]   # existing issue dict
    return []
```

**Also add a regression test.** A blocking lint rule that has never once fired
correctly on real data should not have shipped, and the cheapest guard is a
handful of past `output/*/resume.md` files asserted to produce zero
false-positive structural issues.

### 2.2 — HIGH — The Prospecta discipline floor and the Top Third doctrine are in direct conflict, and the code enforces the losing side

**Files:** `data/background.md:362`, `src/structure_lint.py:54-55`,
`data/resume_best_practices.md` "The Top Third" | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

Calibration Note 362 requires, *in this order*: (1) an SEO bullet, (2) an
Account Executive bullet, (3) then the dev bullets. `structure_lint` enforces
the ordering by only searching `bullets[:3]`.

`resume_best_practices.md` simultaneously says: "The lead entry's first bullet
must be the single most impressive genuinely-supported claim for *this* JD...
Primacy is a real effect; the strongest bullet buried at position four is worth
much less than the same bullet at position one."

For any dev-family JD these two rules cannot both be satisfied. The system
currently resolves the conflict in favor of putting the two least
JD-relevant bullets in the two highest-primacy slots on the page — directly
under the lead employer, which the Ladders eye-tracking study identifies as the
first place a reviewer's eyes land.

**The accumulated evidence says the note's premise is backwards.** Note 362
justifies itself with: "an all-coding Prospecta entry reads as 'is this
candidate an SEO or a software engineer?' to recruiters, which is a real
problem seen on a past resume." Across nine independent hiring-manager reviews,
**not one** reviewer said they couldn't tell what the candidate did because the
SEO work was missing. Nine out of nine said the opposite — that the SEO framing
is what disqualified the engineering narrative. comma.ai stated the causal
chain outright: "the main resume bullets under the primary job are about SEO
strategy and client account ownership, not software engineering scope."

**Recommended change — keep the floor, drop the ordering, for dev-family JDs
only.** The floor's real purpose (don't paint an inaccurate picture of the job)
is served by *presence*, not by *position*.

Replace the ordering clause in Calibration Note 362 with:

> NON-NEGOTIABLE FLOOR, regardless of how dev-focused the target JD is: this
> entry MUST include at least one genuine SEO/technical-SEO bullet and at least
> one genuine Account Executive/client-relationship bullet. **Ordering depends
> on the role family.** For SEO, growth, marketing, and sales JDs, the SEO and
> AE bullets lead. For software-engineering-family JDs, the strongest
> JD-relevant engineering bullet leads and the SEO and AE bullets go **last
> within the entry**, kept to one line each — they are there so the entry
> describes the real job accurately, not to be the first thing a reviewer
> reads. Dropping either to zero bullets remains prohibited in every case.

And in `structure_lint.py`, take the ordering out of the check — search all
bullets in the entry, not `bullets[:3]`:

```python
has_seo = any(_SEO_VOCAB_RE.search(b) for b in bullets)
has_account = any(_ACCOUNT_VOCAB_RE.search(b) for b in bullets)
```

This is the highest-leverage single change available that does not require new
material in `background.md`.

### 2.3 — HIGH — Add a scope line under the official title (offense play, integrity-safe)

**Files:** `data/background.md:360`, `data/resume_best_practices.md` §Section
Structure | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

Two independent research findings converge here:

1. The Ladders 2018 eye-tracking study (30 recruiters, gaze-tracked) found
   reviewers fixate **first on current title and company**, then previous title,
   then dates, then education.
2. ATS vendor behavior documentation reports that Workday "weights job title
   match heavily, so if your previous title does not map onto the target
   title's seniority level, your score drops significantly regardless of your
   skills."

So the string "Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive" is costing this
candidate at both the machine-ranking stage and the first-fixation stage — and
Calibration Note 360 correctly forbids changing it, because a title mismatch
against employer records is a genuine background-check liability. The
recruiting sources agree with Austin on this specific point: "Rewriting the job
title in the experience section may help machine scoring briefly, but it
creates a larger credibility problem when a recruiter or reference check
compares the history against the real record."

But Note 360 also already grants the principle that self-description of scope
is legitimate: "it's fine and encouraged to use a blended display title... in
the resume's tagline/summary since that's self-description, not a claim about
the employer's official title record."

**The lever currently unused: extend that permission from the tagline down to a
scope line inside the Experience entry**, where the eye actually goes.

```
**Prospecta Marketing**
*Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive – Feb 2024-Present*
*Scope: full-stack development, internal tooling, CMS and data-pipeline ownership*
```

The title of record is unchanged and verbatim. The scope line is a factual
self-description of the work, exactly like the tagline, and every word of it is
backed by the `[DEV]` Prospecta section. This puts engineering vocabulary
inside the first fixation zone without touching the integrity boundary.

Add to Note 360, after the existing "blended display title" sentence:

> A separate italicized `*Scope: ...*` line directly beneath the official
> title line is also permitted and encouraged on engineering-family JDs. It is
> self-description of the work performed, not a claim about the employer's
> title record, and it must only name work genuinely documented in the [DEV]
> section. Never merge it into the title line itself.

I considered recommending the industry-standard "Functional Title (internal
title: X)" form, which several recruiting sources prefer. I am not recommending
it here — the risk Austin names is real, the sources themselves flag it, and
the scope line captures most of the benefit with none of the exposure.

### 2.4 — HIGH — The Prospecta floor check passes on any bullet containing the word "client"

**File:** `src/structure_lint.py:15-19` | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

`_ACCOUNT_VOCAB_RE` includes the bare token `client`. Verified against real
bullets:

```
True  ← "Architected a new CMS structure and led a full site redesign for a client"
True  ← "Own the client relationship for Jeff Gray DDS, coordinating with a dev team"
```

Both are dev bullets. Neither is an Account Executive bullet. Since nearly
every bullet in an agency resume contains "client," the AE half of the floor
check is effectively always-true and provides no protection at all. Trivelta
passed the account check on a pure dev-coordination bullet.

**Fix:** require an account-management *activity*, not the noun "client":

```python
_ACCOUNT_VOCAB_RE = re.compile(
    r"\b(account executive|client conference|monthly client|client relationship|"
    r"stakeholder management|point of contact|funnel diagnosis|"
    r"agency relationship|promoted to account|client-facing)\b",
    re.IGNORECASE,
)
```

Same class of problem, worth checking: `_SEO_VOCAB_RE` contains `schema` and
`structured data`, both of which a *frontend* bullet about JSON-LD would match
without being SEO work.

### 2.5 — HIGH — Nothing re-lints after the hiring-manager gate or the shortening pass

**Files:** `src/hiring_gate.py:79-84`, `src/length_fit.py:51-53`,
`main.py:63-86` | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

The supervisor loop lints on every iteration. Then two later stages mutate the
draft with **no lint feedback at all**:

- `hiring_gate.run()` calls `draft_agent.revise(...)` and then `length_fit`,
  then loops straight back to the HM review. A revision here can freely
  reintroduce em dashes, gap confessions, backticks, Kharon-in-the-resume, or
  break the Prospecta floor.
- `length_fit.enforce_one_page()` calls `draft_agent.shorten(...)` with no
  structural constraints. This has already caused a documented Calibration Note
  violation — Clerkie (Jul 26) tailoring notes record: *"[shortening, attempt
  2] Removed the DNVR/PHNX entry entirely from Tangent Apps"* — which is
  precisely what Note 364 exists to prevent, and Note 364 even predicts the
  exact failure ("shortening passes have repeatedly cut it first on
  quantification grounds — don't").

`main.py` stage 5 catches the damage but **only reports it**. There is no
repair path. Given Austin's stated goal, a diagnostic-only final stage
guarantees he stays the QA layer.

**Three fixes, in order of value:**

1. **Lint inside the hiring gate.** In `hiring_gate.py`, after the
   `length_fit` call, compute `style_lint.lint_draft(draft) +
   structure_lint.lint_draft(draft, background_subset)` and fold those issues
   into the *next* `revise()` call's gaps list alongside the HM concerns.

2. **Constrain `shorten()`.** Pass the current structure_lint result into
   `draft_agent.shorten()` and add to its user message an explicit
   never-cut list:

   > Hard constraints on what you may NOT cut, regardless of length pressure:
   > the Prospecta entry's SEO bullet and Account Executive bullet; the
   > Basecamp AI Agent bullet; and the DNVR/PHNX and Lacroix/Drillhouse
   > bullets, which may be compressed to one line each but never removed. If
   > the resume still does not fit after everything else has been tightened,
   > compress these to one line rather than deleting them.

3. **Add a bounded repair pass at stage 5.** If `final_style_issues` is
   non-empty, run one `draft_agent.revise()` with only those issues, re-run
   `length_fit`, and re-lint once. Cap at one attempt. This converts the most
   common `manual_review` cause into a self-healing step.

### 2.6 — MEDIUM — There is no code path that can shorten a cover letter

**Files:** `src/length_fit.py`, `src/draft_agent.py:357-392` | **Tag:
prompt/process fix**

`draft_agent.shorten()` explicitly says "Keep the cover letter exactly as-is —
return it unchanged." The only mechanism that can shorten a cover letter is
`revise()` reacting to the new `structure_lint` word-count issue. Given a 7/7
historical violation rate at an average of 428 words, this will now be a
recurring blocking issue with a weak remediation path.

**Fix:** add the word budget to the draft prompt as a hard, up-front
constraint rather than a post-hoc lint. In `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS`, replace the
current cover-letter guidance with:

> Cover letter hard limit: 350 words. This is enforced mechanically after you
> submit, and a letter that has to be cut down reads worse than one written to
> length. Before submitting, count the words in your cover letter. If it is
> over 330, cut the weakest of your two concrete examples down rather than
> trimming adjectives evenly across the whole letter.

Also drop `COVER_LETTER_WORD_LIMIT` enforcement severity to `medium` only if
you adopt the repair pass in 2.5.3; otherwise it is another guaranteed
`manual_review`.

### 2.7 — MEDIUM — Add deterministic backstops for the two Calibration Notes that keep getting ignored

**File:** `src/structure_lint.py` | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

Both of these are prompt-only rules the model has already broken on real runs.
Per the "restating a rule the model already had is hope, not a fix" principle,
they need code.

**(a) Banned contrast-tone constructions (Note 375, violated in the newest run):**

```python
_CONTRAST_TONE_RE = re.compile(
    r"\bnot (a|just a|merely a)? ?(demo|tutorial|toy|prototype|proof[- ]of[- ]concept|"
    r"side project|hobby project|sample|exercise)\b"
    r"|\bunlike a (toy|demo|tutorial)\b"
    r"|\brather than a (demo|tutorial|toy|proof[- ]of[- ]concept)\b",
    re.IGNORECASE,
)
```

Issue text: "Bullet is phrased as a contrast against a hypothetical lesser
version of itself, which reads as defensive. State the real thing plainly —
'live in production, used daily' carries the same information without the tone
problem." Severity `high`, applied to both resume and cover letter.

**(b) Differentiator-bullet default (Note 364, ignored in 5 of 11 runs):**

```python
def _check_differentiator_defaults(resume: str) -> list[dict]:
    lowered = resume.lower()
    missing = []
    if "dnvr" not in lowered and "phnx" not in lowered:
        missing.append("DNVR/PHNX (48-hour AWS launch crisis)")
    if "lacroix" not in lowered and "drillhouse" not in lowered:
        missing.append("Lacroix Hockey / Drillhouse Sports Center")
    if not missing:
        return []
    return [{
        "category": "Structure - Differentiator Default",
        "detail": (
            f"Missing from the Tangent Apps entry: {', '.join(missing)}. "
            "Calibration Notes mark these as default-included across nearly all "
            "resumes — their value is adaptability and client communication "
            "under pressure, not technical relevance, so JD-relevance and "
            "quantification are the wrong reasons to cut them. Compress to one "
            "line before removing."
        ),
        "severity": "low",
    }]
```

Keep this at `low` severity — it is a strong default, not a floor, and Note 364
explicitly permits trimming under real page pressure. If you adopt the stage-5
repair pass, consider excluding `low`-severity issues from the `accepted`
gate so soft defaults don't block a shipment.

### 2.8 — MEDIUM — The HM gate can essentially never pass on a dev-family JD, and the status model hides that

**Files:** `src/hiring_gate.py:58-74`, `main.py:76-86` | **Tag: architectural**

100% of dev-family runs produce at least one `structural` seniority concern.
When *every* concern is structural, `hiring_gate` correctly stops early — and
returns `passed=False`, which forces `final_status = "manual_review"`.

That is correct behavior wrapped in a misleading label. "Manual review" tells
Austin to go fix something. But `stopped_reason` already says, accurately, that
"no revision was attempted because no rewrite of these documents would change
it." Those two messages contradict each other, and the second one is right.

**Fix:** introduce a third terminal status.

```python
structurally_capped = (
    not hm_gate_result.passed
    and hm_gate_result.stopped_reason  # only set on the all-structural path
    and final_score_result.score >= config.SCORE_THRESHOLD
    and not final_score_result.integrity_flags
    and not final_style_issues
    and length_result.fits_one_page
)
```

Report it as `accepted_structurally_capped` with a one-line explanation: *this
packet is as strong as these two documents can be made; what's left is a fact
about your history, and the fix is new material in background.md or a different
target role, not another draft.* That is genuinely actionable, where
"manual_review" is not.

### 2.9 — MEDIUM — The score agent is not discriminating

**File:** `config.py:22` | **Tag: architectural**

Across 10 runs the score took four distinct values: 72 (×4), 78 (×4), 87, 88.
Eight of ten runs landed on exactly 72 or 78. A 0-100 scale collapsing to two
values across ten materially different drafts is not measuring anything, and
`SCORE_THRESHOLD = 85` is therefore effectively a coin flip on Haiku's mood
rather than a quality bar.

Two options, in order of preference:

1. Move `SCORE_MODEL` to Sonnet for the *final* verification call only (stage 5
   in `main.py`), keeping Haiku for the in-loop iterations where cost matters.
   The final call is the one that decides `accepted` vs `manual_review`.
2. Alternatively, stop gating on the numeric score entirely and gate on
   `integrity_flags == 0 and lint == 0 and fits_one_page and HM verdict`. The
   score's real value is the `gaps` and `strengths` it produces for the report,
   not the number.

### 2.10 — MEDIUM — Make the education line stop reading as "still a student"

**File:** `data/resume_best_practices.md` §Section Structure item 5 | **Tag:
prompt/process fix**

Seven of nine reviewers stumbled on the degree line, several concluding
"March 2026 is in the future" in an August 2026 run. Fivetran connected it
straight to the seniority verdict: the degree plus the in-progress AWS cert
"read as someone still building foundational credentials, reinforcing the
junior-for-this-role signal."

Two concrete changes, neither of which touches a fact:

- Write the degree as `B.S. Computer Science — Western Governors University,
  2026`. A bare completion year is the standard convention and does not invite
  a month-arithmetic error. "graduated March 2026" and "completed March 2026"
  both read as an event still in flight to a fast skimmer.
- On senior-band JDs, drop `AWS Solutions Architect – Associate (in progress)`
  entirely. `resume_best_practices.md` already says in-progress credentials
  must stay out of the top third; the evidence says on a senior req they should
  stay off the page. It buys nothing (an unearned cert is not a keyword any ATS
  scores) and it costs a documented seniority deduction. Keep it on junior and
  mid-band JDs, where "actively certifying" reads as trajectory.

Suggested addition to the Section Structure item:

> Education line format: `B.S. Computer Science — Western Governors University,
> 2026`. Use the completion year alone, not a month — a month-precision date on
> a recent degree repeatedly gets misread as in-progress. In-progress
> certifications are included on junior and mid-band reqs and **omitted
> entirely on senior/lead reqs**, where they read as building foundational
> credentials rather than as ongoing investment.

### 2.11 — LOW — "Training to take over CTO responsibilities" needs grounding or cutting

**File:** `data/background.md` Calibration Notes | **Tag: prompt/process fix**

The phrase appears in 9 of 11 resumes and drew an explicit HM objection:
Trivelta — "`[medium] seniority_calibration`: 'Now training to take over CTO
responsibilities' at a small marketing agency is a vague, unverifiable claim
that oversells scope relative to demonstrated evidence (fixable)."

It is true, and it is exactly the kind of claim reviewers discount: a promise

## 3. External research notes

### 3.1 The 6-second scan — confirmed, and more specific than the current doc assumes

The Ladders 2018 eye-tracking study (gaze-tracked professional recruiters,
follow-up to their 2012 study) puts the initial screen at **7.4 seconds**, and
— more usefully — reports the actual gaze path: *current title and company
first, then the previous title, then dates, then education.*

`resume_best_practices.md` §"The Top Third" currently describes this correctly
but draws the wrong operational conclusion from it. It optimizes the **tagline
and Summary**, which is the part of the page the study says recruiters reach
*after* the title/company fixation. The title line inside the Experience
section is the first thing seen, and for this candidate it is the single worst
string on the page. That finding is what recommendation 2.3 is built on.

### 3.2 ATS behavior — title weighting is the underrated finding

Current ATS documentation converges on three points relevant here:

- A dedicated skills section is a "concentrated keyword zone" and self-declared
  skills there carry real extraction weight — which supports keeping a Core
  Skills block, as the house style does.
- **Workday weights job-title match heavily**, and a previous title that
  doesn't map to the target title's seniority drops the score "significantly
  regardless of your skills." Greenhouse does not auto-score at all; Lever
  front-loads human review.
- Matching is *relative to the applicant pool*, not against a fixed threshold.

The practical implication: the house style's existing "placement beats
frequency" rule is sound, but the biggest single ATS lever for this candidate
is the title string, which cannot be changed. That reinforces targeting
Greenhouse/Lever-stack companies (smaller, startup-heavy) over Workday-stack
enterprises — which happens to align with the four "advance" verdicts.

- [How Workday, Greenhouse & Taleo Read Your Resume (2026)](https://www.shashiworks.com/ats-workday-greenhouse-taleo.html)
- [ATS Job Title Mismatch: Why It Hurts Score](https://www.profileops.com/en/blog/ats-job-title-mismatch)
- [Why Your ATS Score Is a Lie: How Keyword Matching Actually Works](https://jobloo.co/blog/ats-score-keyword-matching-explained/)

### 3.3 Cover letters — worth the 350 words

Survey data supports the current investment: ~83% of hiring managers report
reading cover letters even when optional, and the split between recruiters
(~40-52% skip) and final decision-makers (~23% skip) matters — the person who
decides is the one who reads. Notably, 45% report reading the letter *before*
the resume.

For this candidate specifically that is unusually valuable, because the letter
is where the career-path reconciliation actually lands. Three separate HM
reviews credited it: Clerkie (Aug 5) — "This is the rare case where the cover
letter actually does the work of contextualizing an odd-looking resume."

Caveat on source quality: these are vendor-run surveys with obvious incentive
alignment (resume companies benefit from "cover letters matter"). Treat the
direction as reliable and the precise percentages as soft.

### 3.4 Breadth vs. depth — the verdict

**Question:** is `resume_best_practices.md`'s standing "baseline breadth line"
(WordPress/PHP + Webflow + Wix + SquareSpace on every resume regardless of JD)
right, too conservative, or already too much?

**First, an honest note on the evidence base:** this rule postdates every run in
`output/`. The Webflow/Wix/SquareSpace platform list appears in exactly one
artifact — `general-resume-indeed/resume.md`, which was hand-made, not
generated. **There is zero empirical data in this repository about the rule's
effect.** Anyone claiming the rejection history validates or refutes it is
reading tea leaves. What follows is external evidence plus reasoning, labeled
as such.

**Where the sources converge (four independent source types, same direction):**

1. *T-shaped is the target, and the crossbar must be visibly subordinate.* A
   technical recruiting firm's reviewer guidance states it as a screening rule:
   "Equal emphasis across all technologies is a red flag. If every technology is
   listed as 'expert' or 'advanced,' none of them probably are."
2. *Flat platform lists actively destroy the depth signal.* A first-person
   account from a reviewer of 400+ tech resumes: "A skills section that lists 45
   technologies provides no signal about depth," and "Listing Python is
   universal and tells the reviewer nothing. Listing Python with specific
   mention of async frameworks, type annotation systems, and profiling tools
   tells the reviewer something."
3. *Breadth belongs, but positioned low and framed as compatible-adjacent.*
   Monster's guidance is "specialist, with breadth" — depth on the JD's needed
   skills, compatible skills at the bottom — and warns that a "hodgepodge
   resume is more likely to confuse hiring managers who may think of you as a
   dabbler without depth."
4. *Never list what you can't defend.* Repeated across sources, and already the
   house rule in `resume_best_practices.md` and Calibration Note 377.

**Where seniority and company stage change the answer.** Early-stage startups
genuinely do want range — the "T-shaped first hires" convention is well
established, and Austin's target market (Series A+) sits right at the
transition point that the startup-hiring sources describe: high-agency
generalists for the first hires, "targeted specialists" as complexity rises
post-Series-A. So breadth is an asset in his target market — but it is an asset
at the *conversation* stage, not the *screening* stage, and only when the
breadth is expressed in systems shipped rather than platforms touched.

**Where the sources genuinely disagree:** Monster says put compatible breadth
at the bottom of the resume; the 400-resume reviewer and the recruiting-firm
guidance say prune aggressively. My read is that this is a disagreement about
*form*, not *principle* — all three would reject a co-equal list of website
builders, and all three would accept "shipped production systems in three
different stacks."

**One source I am explicitly discounting:** The Interview Guys' widely-cited
"repositioning from generalist to specialist raised interview rate from 1 in 47
to 1 in 12" is a self-reported internal test on 50 job seekers with no
published methodology, no controls, and no seniority breakdown. It points the
same direction as everything else, which is why I mention it, but it should
carry no weight on its own.

**Verdict: the instinct is right, the implementation is the weakest available
form of it, and on engineering-family JDs it is currently a net negative.**

Austin's premise — that demonstrated versatility across different kinds of
production work is itself a differentiator — is correct and well supported for
his target market. But the current rule cashes that premise out as *a list of
platform names, two of which are website builders.* "Webflow, Wix, SquareSpace"
is a tool-familiarity list, which is precisely the artifact every source
identifies as the *anti*-signal: it reads as dabbling, it provides no depth
information, and on a senior frontend or full-stack req it actively imports the
"is this a marketing person?" frame that already appears in 9 of 9 rejections.
Adding it to a resume that is already losing on `narrative_coherence` 100% of
the time is pouring fuel on the one fire that is actually burning.

WordPress/PHP is a different case and should stay. It is backed by two
from-scratch full-stack plugins with real schema design, indexed tables, a
REST-style API, nonce/prepared-statement security work, and an admin dashboard.
That is depth, not a builder, and it is defensible in a technical screen.

**The exact replacement.** Delete the current "Baseline breadth line" paragraph
in `resume_best_practices.md` §Section Structure item 3 and replace it with:

> **Baseline breadth — express it in stacks shipped, not platforms touched
> (revised per 2026-08-06 v2 audit).** Versatility is a genuine differentiator
> for this candidate and should be visible on every resume, but it must be
> expressed as *production systems delivered in different stacks*, never as a
> list of platform names. The defensible breadth claim is: PHP/MySQL full-stack
> plugin architecture, React/Node/Express with Stripe payments, and
> Python/AWS serverless — three different production stacks, each with a real
> shipped artifact behind it. Put that breadth in the Core Skills category
> labels and let the Experience bullets prove it.
>
> - **WordPress/PHP always stays.** It is backed by two from-scratch full-stack
>   plugins (custom schema, REST-style AJAX API, nonce and prepared-statement
>   security, admin dashboard). That is depth, and it is defensible in a
>   technical screen.
> - **Webflow, Wix, and SquareSpace are role-family gated.** Include them on
>   SEO, growth, marketing, and agency JDs, where they are genuinely relevant
>   and where the Honeycomb SquareSpace engagement and the Webflow CMS rebuild
>   are real credentials. **Omit them entirely on software-engineering-family
>   JDs.** On an engineering req they are the lowest-signal items on the page:
>   they read as website-builder work rather than engineering, and they
>   reinforce exactly the generalist/marketing framing that has driven the
>   `narrative_coherence` and `seniority_calibration` concerns on every
>   dev-family application to date.
> - The Webflow *CMS rebuild* for Jeff Gray, DDS is a legitimate engineering
>   bullet on its own merits (architecture, ownership, coordinating with a
>   client's senior developer) and may appear in Experience on any JD. That is
>   different from listing "Webflow" as a platform skill — describe the work,
>   not the tool.

**On the current rule's self-defeating escape hatch.** The existing paragraph
ends: "If a hiring-manager review specifically flags narrative incoherence or
'generalist vs. specialist' as a concern, that's a signal to trim this line
back for that application." That escape hatch is unreachable. The HM gate runs
at stage 4, after the draft is written; its concerns feed `revise()` but never
persist back into the rule; and `narrative_coherence` has already fired on 9 of
9 applications, meaning the trigger condition is permanently met and the rule
still never trims. A conditional rule whose condition is always true and whose
consequence never fires is not a calibration, it's a comment. Replacing it with
the role-family gate above makes the decision at draft time, deterministically,
which is where it belongs.

## 4. Where I disagree

**4.1 — Calibration Note 362's ordering requirement is wrong for dev-family
JDs, and its stated justification is contradicted by this system's own data.**
The note says an all-coding Prospecta entry "reads as 'is this candidate an SEO
or a software engineer?' to recruiters." Across nine independent
hiring-manager reviews, zero raised that. Nine raised the opposite. The note is
protecting against a hypothetical failure while causing a measured one. Keep
the floor (it is honest and it prevents an inaccurate picture of the job),
drop the ordering on engineering JDs. Full replacement text in §2.2.

**4.2 — The baseline breadth line is Austin's stated preference and I'm
recommending it be materially changed.** Not deleted — the underlying instinct
about versatility is right and well supported for Series A+ targets. But
"Webflow, Wix, SquareSpace" on a senior frontend req is the single
easiest-to-cut thing on the page and it feeds the one objection that has fired
on 100% of applications. Express breadth as three shipped production stacks
instead. Full replacement text in §3.4. I want to be clear this is a real
disagreement with a real preference, offered because the evidence supports it,
not a softened restatement.

**4.3 — I disagree with the prior audit's decision to raise `FIT_GATE_MIN_SCORE`
to 70.** The config comment justifies it: "every historical run scored 62-78%,
so 50 had never once fired." But raising the threshold to 70 means the gate now
fires on nearly every run, and its only action is an interactive
`input()` prompt. A gate that always fires is a gate that gets reflexively
answered "y," which is worse than no gate — it trains the operator to dismiss
it. The gate's *useful* signal is `disqualifying_requirements`, which is
already checked independently and is genuinely discriminating (it correctly
flagged the Hims & Hers 5+ years and the trading-platform C++/MT5 cases). I'd
put `FIT_GATE_MIN_SCORE` back to ~55 and rely on `disqualifying_requirements`
plus `red_flags` as the real trip conditions.

**4.4 — I disagree with treating all lint severities as equally blocking.**
`main.py:76-86` requires `not final_style_issues` — any issue at any severity
forces `manual_review`, including `low` ones like the resume word-count
precheck, which is explicitly advisory ("Consider tightening now to save a
shortening round-trip"). An advisory hint should not be able to block a
shipment. Gate `accepted` on `high` and `medium` issues only.

**4.5 — Partial disagreement on where I did *not* push.** I considered
recommending the standard recruiting convention of a translated functional
title with the official title in parentheses ("Full-Stack Engineer (official
title: Senior SEO Specialist & Account Executive)"). Multiple recruiting sources
recommend exactly this. I'm not recommending it, and I want to record why:
Austin's Calibration Note 360 identifies the background-check risk, and the
sources themselves confirm it ("creates a larger credibility problem when a
recruiter or reference check compares the history against the real record").
On this one Austin's instinct is better calibrated than the generic advice, and
the scope-line variant in §2.3 captures most of the upside with none of the
exposure. Noting it so a future audit doesn't re-litigate it from scratch.

**4.6 — Nothing to disagree with on the fabrication line.** Every guardrail
around it — the "Real Numbers Only" section's explicit rejection of
"conservative estimation," Note 377's treatment of the tech-stack index as
non-claims, the score agent's two-way date check, the Kharon containment rule —
is correctly designed and, as far as I can tell from the outputs, working. I
looked for a place where the integrity rules were costing more than they were
worth and did not find one.

## 5. What's already working well

Do not change these.

1. **`style_lint.GAP_CONFESSION_PHRASES` is the best thing in this codebase.**
   I ran the current lint against the five historical drafts that had
   gap-confession problems; it catches every one, including the third-person
   workarounds ("ramping up on," "comfortable picking up," "eager to learn")
   that the model invented after the first-person forms were banned. This is
   the correct pattern — a prompt rule the model routed around, then closed
   mechanically — and it should be the template for §2.7.

2. **The `fixable` / `structural` split on hiring-manager concerns.** Correctly
   conceived and correctly implemented, including the default-to-fixable on a
   missing tag so nothing silently vanishes. It is the reason the pipeline
   stops instead of burning a Sonnet call trying to rewrite the candidate's
   employment history. My only change is to the *status* it produces (§2.8),
   not the logic.

3. **`_quality_key` / best-draft tracking in the supervisor.** Shipping the
   best iteration rather than the last is right, and the code comment
   explaining *why* (free-running revision degrades clean drafts) reflects a
   real observed failure mode. Trivelta's report shows it working: "shipped
   iteration 3 of 3."

4. **`length_fit` rendering an actual `.docx` and asking Word for the true page
   count** instead of guessing from word count. This is the single most
   over-engineered-looking decision in the repo and it is completely correct —
   word-count heuristics for one-page fit are unreliable, and the
   `RESUME_WORD_PRECHECK_LIMIT` advisory is properly labeled as a hint rather
   than a substitute.

5. **The hiring manager not receiving `background.md`.** Deliberately
   withholding ground truth so it evaluates only what a real reviewer sees is
   the right architecture, and the docstring defending it is right that giving
   it ground truth "would just make it a worse copy of score_agent." The
   quality of the concerns it produces — specific, quotable, correctly
   categorized — validates the choice.

6. **The malformed-output recovery in `hiring_manager.py`.** The
   `<positive_signals>` leak handler, the multi-format `_CONCERN_RE`
   alternation, the newline-splitting in `as_gap_dicts`, and the
   degenerate-response retry are all responses to real observed failures, and
   the Trivelta report shows the leak handler catching a live instance.

7. **The Kharon containment rules.** Note 361 plus the score-agent hard check
   plus `structure_lint._check_kharon_containment` is exactly the belt-and-
   suspenders treatment a fabrication-adjacent risk deserves, and no run has
   leaked it into a resume.

8. **`_check_no_backticks`.** Added today; would have fired on 6 of 7 past
   resumes (`$wpdb`, `dbDelta`, `wp_ajax_*`, `generateMetadata`,
   `MAX(CASE WHEN...)`). Real, high-frequency problem, correctly closed. Keep.

9. **The tailoring-notes trail.** It is what made this audit possible — the
   Clerkie (Jul 26) note recording that a shortening pass deleted the DNVR
   entry is the kind of evidence most pipelines throw away. Keep writing them
   to the report.
