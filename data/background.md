**SURFACING RULE for the line above (added 2026-08-22 after a real, shipped miss; NARROWED 2026-08-23 after it over-fired).** When a JD states a citizenship requirement, a clearance requirement (Public Trust, Secret, TS/SCI, "federal clearance", "ability to obtain a clearance"), or says it will not sponsor work authorization, the resume MUST state the true status explicitly — do not leave it to be inferred.

**This rule fires ONLY on those three triggers. Do not include it otherwise.** On the 2026-08-23 Runpod run (a commercial AI-infrastructure company, remote, no clearance or citizenship language anywhere in the posting) the resume shipped a "Work Authorization: U.S. citizen, clearance-eligible" line purely because the requirement list mentioned a background check. **A routine background check is NOT a trigger** — nearly every employer runs one, and clearance-eligibility language on an ordinary commercial req is off-key filler that spends a line of a one-page resume saying nothing the reader asked about. Absent one of the three real triggers, say nothing about work authorization at all. On the 2026-08-22 SMX run (a federal contractor role listing "Public Trust" and "U.S. citizenship required" under Required Skills), neither citizenship nor clearance eligibility appeared anywhere in the resume or cover letter, and the simulated hiring manager rejected partly on that, calling it "a required, non-negotiable qualification" left unaddressed and marking it FIXABLE. This is unusual among gaps in that the true fact is genuinely favorable and simply went unsaid — silence here costs an application for no reason.

Exact permitted phrasing, and the boundary: write "U.S. citizen; eligible for Public Trust clearance" or "U.S. citizen, clearance-eligible." NEVER write or imply that a clearance is currently held, active, or in process — it is not, and a clearance claim is verified directly against federal records during onboarding, making it among the most consequential possible fabrications in this entire document. If a JD requires a clearance to *already be active* as a condition of applying, that is a genuine disqualifying gap: say nothing beyond the true citizenship/eligibility status, and never bridge the difference with softening language. Place it on the contact/header line or as a short Education & Certifications line, not as a Core Skills entry or a bullet.
