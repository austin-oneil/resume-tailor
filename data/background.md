## [PROJECTS] Take-Home Technical Exercise — Kharon (Senior Software Engineer, interview process, 2026)

Not employment — reached the final rounds of Kharon's interview process (6 interviews total, including the chief of engineering, senior devs, and VP of marketing) for a Senior Software Engineer role, and only didn't advance to the final round because the other two finalist candidates had more years of SaaS experience. The take-home itself got a perfect score from the engineers who reviewed it.

- Completed a timeboxed (60–90 min) Next.js 15 / React 19 / TypeScript take-home replatforming a news site's reader-facing app against a WordPress-compatible headless CMS (REST API, `_embed`, pagination, realistic latency simulation)
- **Article detail page**: built the dynamic route (`/brief/[slug]`) from a documented spec — slug-based fetch, full article render (headline, byline, publish date, hero image, body), and complete SEO surface: dynamic `<title>`/meta description, OpenGraph tags (including `publishedTime`), and JSON-LD `NewsArticle` structured data (headline, dates, author, image)
- Diagnosed that a provided root-layout `SiteHeader` reads cookies (session state), which forces the entire route tree to dynamic rendering regardless of per-page choice — rather than fight the constraint, re-architected the caching strategy around it
- Implemented a cached data-access layer (`lib/wp.ts`) using Next.js's `fetch` cache with a 60-second `revalidate` window (stale-while-revalidate pattern), cutting article load time from ~860ms cold to ~50ms warm while keeping the CMS off the hot path per-request
- Documented the production-scale answer in writing: server-render dynamic today, but at 100x traffic move to Partial Prerendering (PPR) — prerender the shell/gate teaser statically for crawlers and logged-out readers, stream only the signed-in upgrade, noting PPR was canary-only in the shipped Next.js version and therefore out of scope for the exercise itself
- **Archive gating logic (Part B)**: implemented server-side gating for articles older than 8 months — anonymous visitors see a teaser component, signed-in readers see the full body — and made the deliberate call to keep the gate check server-side and stateless (a pure function of public `date_gmt` vs. a session lookup), so the full article body never reaches anonymous users or crawlers in the HTML payload at all, rather than hiding it client-side
- Reasoned through and documented an edge case unprompted: an article crossing the 8-month threshold at 3am with no publish event to hook into — explained why dynamic per-request age-checking self-heals this with no cron job or webhook required, vs. the staleness window a fully static approach would introduce
- Designed a publish-to-live latency story: explained the up-to-60-second propagation delay from the revalidate window, proposed an on-publish WordPress webhook calling `revalidatePath()` for instant invalidation with the timer as a backstop, and reasoned about tuning revalidation windows per content type (breaking news vs. evergreen)
- Added a `Post` type covering only the WordPress fields the app actually consumes (rejecting `any`), trading strict typing for the maintenance cost of extending the type when new fields are needed — called out explicitly as a tradeoff, not an oversight
- Made and documented a security-relevant judgment call: the seed CMS served draft/scheduled posts to anonymous requests (a bug in the stand-in), and rather than patch the CMS (out of scope per the brief), enforced visibility filtering (`status === "publish"` and `date_gmt <= now`) on the reader side — reasoning that the consuming app owning that invariant is the safer default regardless of what the upstream source returns
- Wrote a WordPress HTML-entity/shortcode cleaner (`cleanWP`) to normalize `&nbsp;`, `&amp;`, numeric character entities, and WP's `[&hellip;]` read-more marker for clean meta descriptions and schema output — flagged as intentionally partial given the timebox
- Built `/sitemap.xml` generation (the optional stretch goal) reusing the same visibility-filtered data fetch as the homepage, so unpublished/future-dated posts can never leak into search engine discovery
- Delivered a `DECISIONS.md` addressing rendering-strategy tradeoffs, cache-invalidation latency, an edge-case walkthrough, and self-identified known gaps (weak auto-generated excerpts, unpaginated homepage fetch, a session-read architecture constraint left in place per instructions) — including a note on which functions (slug resolution, visibility filtering, archive-date logic) would be unit tested first as the highest-risk pure-function seam
- Left `// DECISION:` inline comments at each judgment-call site in the codebase per the exercise's requirement, anchoring a live technical conversation about the tradeoffs during the follow-up interview round
